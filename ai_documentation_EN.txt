About this document

Background
Paragon's AI architecture was split into two layers: the Micro Brain (native Behavior Trees + EQS, handling combat, ability usage, and local decision-making) and the Macro Brain (the OrionAICommander + BotObjectiveGraph, handling strategic lane assignments, objective calls, and team coordination). While the Micro Brain runs entirely engine-side and works on a listen-server, the Macro Brain depended on Epic's backend matchmaking servers, which delivered the BotObjectiveGraph at match start. Without that backend, the Commander wakes up, finds no objective graph, and the bots fall into a permanent idle state after clearing their local lane wave.
The AI Commander Emulator rebuilds the missing Macro Brain by writing directly to UE4 object memory offsets — injecting BotObjective instances, Behavior Tree assignments, and EQS overrides into the same native fields the original Commander would have populated. The native Micro Brain (Behavior Trees, EQS, ability casting, target selection) remains completely untouched. The ai code acts purely as a "strategic dispatcher" that gives bots their macro-level orders, then steps back and lets the native Epic AI handle execution.

Core Architecture

Minimal Intervention Philosophy: the ai code only writes to the fields the dead Commander would have written (CurrentObjective at 0x0748, ObjectiveBT at 0x50, RelatedActor at 0xA0, GoalActor at 0x130, AICommander backlink at 0x128). All combat, ability usage, pathfinding, and micro-positioning remain 100% native Epic AI.
Objective Recycling System: Instead of creating new BotObjective instances every tick, the emulator checks whether the bot's current objective already matches the desired type (WholeLane, AttackJungleCamp, Gank, Recall) and reuses it. New objectives are only instantiated via StaticConstructObject when the type changes.
Commander Hijack Firewall: Detects when the native (non-functional) Commander overwrites -assigned objectives and immediately reverts them. Uses a per-bot LastKnownObjective tracker with GC-safe validation (IsValidUE4Object + IsA class check) to prevent stale pointer writes.
Playbook Infrastructure: On initialization, the emulator locates the live OrionAICommander instance, patches the abstract BotPlaybook class flag to allow instantiation, and fills all 10 role playbook slots (5 roles × 2 teams) with valid BotPlaybook instances. The Commander's Members array is then zeroed and its count set to 0, preventing the native Commander from interfering with bot assignments.
Shadow Bot Isolation: Bots created for deck-harvesting ("Frankenstein Deck" system) are teleported to Z=8000, made invulnerable, collision-free, and hidden. They are filtered out of all AI processing via TeamNum == NoTeam early-exit checks.


Role-Based Lane Assignment

5-Role System: Offlane (0), Midlane (1), Safelane Carry (2), Safelane Support (3), Jungle (4) — matching Paragon's ELaneRoleSlot enum.
Mirrored Lane Mapping: Blue team's Offlane maps to physical left (lane 0), Red team's Offlane maps to physical right (lane 2), correctly reflecting Paragon's asymmetric Monolith map layout. [Superseded note: laneEnum is set team-mirrored but never read; the effective botPhysLane is NOT mirrored (roleSlot 0 -> lane 0 for both teams, roles 2/3 -> lane 2), see botPhysLane at dllmain.cpp:5478-5480 and "Matchup Pin" in the addendum.]
Dynamic Lane Override: During lategame (>25 minutes or any inhibitor destroyed), lane restrictions are lifted (laneEnum = 9 / Any) to enable group pushes on the weakest enemy lane.


Laner AI (Offlane / Mid / Carry / Support)
Tower Progression

Identifies the lowest-tier enemy tower on the bot's assigned lane and sets it as the attack target.
Tier priority: Outer (1) → Inner (2) → Inhibitor (3) → Core (4).
Lategame override: Standing inhibitors are re-prioritized to tier 0 (highest attack priority), enabling "skip and finish" strategies.
V45 Win Condition: Bots push the Core when ≥2 inhibitors are down, or when 1 inhibitor is down and ≥2 enemies are dead — without needing all three inhibitors destroyed.

Minion Escort & Safe Pushing

Bots escort the nearest friendly minion wave (within 25m) toward the enemy tower rather than blindly rushing.
The native K2_IsSafeToApproachOffensiveLaneGoal check is respected: if unsafe, the bot waits near the tower edge (1800 acceptance radius) or falls back to escort minions — UNLESS the competitive Tower-Siege override (bSiegeSafe, see below) is satisfied, in which case the bot commits into tower range like a real player.
Bots caught inside an enemy turret range without safe push conditions normally flee to the nearest friendly tower (legacy turret-escape). NOTE: on a listen-server the native AOrionAIBot turret-detection (the IsInsideTurret native call) never returns true (same dead-backend class of problem as K2_IsSafeToApproach), so this legacy path is effectively inert — deep-retreat is instead handled by the distance-based Siege-Abort (see below).


Competitive Tower-Siege Emulation  — Listen-Server Damage + Approach

WHY THIS EXISTS — native solution is impossible on a listen-server:
A real Paragon player damages a tower only from inside the tower's attack radius while their minion wave soaks tower aggro. The native bot AI never generates a damage event against a structure (UOrionDamage::Execute is never raised by bots toward an AOrionDamageableObjective_Base), AND the native K2_IsSafeToApproachOffensiveLaneGoal / IsInsideTurret APIs depend on the dead Epic backend and permanently return "unsafe / not-inside" on a listen-server. Consequently the unmodified bot can neither decide it is safe to dive a tower, nor deal any damage to it. No combination of native fields reproduces this — it must be emulated. Both halves of competitive tower play (the approach decision and the actual damage) are therefore reconstructed in the emulator.

(1) Damage emulation (Stage B): When a blue laner is in a TowerObjective/Attack state, within range of an enemy structure, with no real enemy nearby, an allied minion sieging the structure, and all survival/threat guards clear, the emulator injects a real damage event via the engine's own GameplayAbility path: AbilitySystemBlueprintLibrary.MakeSpecHandle (GE_Minion_Damage_Ranged, bot as Instigator) + AbilitySystemComponent.BP_ApplyGameplayEffectSpecToTarget (bot-ASC → tower-ASC). This flows through the project's existing DoDamagePipeline, so the structure dies with a proper death cascade (no "zombie tower"). Damage is calibrated low (Ranged ~40 / Melee ~50 per BaseAttackTime, ~4 per apply) so towers fall realistically, not instantly. The native Micro Brain, targeting, and animation are untouched (the bot does not visibly swing — a known, accepted cosmetic limitation; game-functionally the tower takes correct damage). [Superseded note: the team gate was removed (Entwurf Q4); Stage B now applies to laners of BOTH teams, see "Stage-B Tower Damage for Both Teams" in the addendum.]

(2) Approach emulation ( bSiegeSafe + SiegeAbort): The legacy "wait at 18m aggro edge" (C-WaitAtTower) kept the bot permanently OUT of tower range, so Stage B rarely fired. bSiegeSafe is a strictly-restrictive per-tick gate: it is true only when ALL of these hold — not lane-push-vetoed, no real enemy in range, not V44-threatened, not boss/heal/recall, HP > 55%, an enemy structure < ~40m, no enemy hero MIA within 50m, and ≥1 friendly lane-minion within 25m of the structure (the aggro soak). When true, the bot re-uses the engine-intended ATTACK movement (escort-minion accR=300 / structure accR=800) and commits into tower range; the legacy turret-flee is suppressed for that bot only. The moment bSiegeSafe drops (wave dies, enemy appears, etc.) AND the bot is still deep (< 30m to the structure), the Siege-Abort branch fires an immediate active retreat to the nearest friendly tower (accR=1500, bIsFleeing=true) — distance-based, because the native turret-detection is dead. This is the competitive "press the advantage, back off when the siege window closes" behavior.

Collision safety: bSiegeSafe/SiegeAbort are purely additive branches inside the existing laner push logic. Every existing subsystem (Wave-Outpace-Veto, V44, Anti-Kite, Recall/Survival, Boss, Rotation, Hijack-Firewall) is represented as a negative term or structurally precedes the gate; when any is active bSiegeSafe is false and the original behavior runs unchanged. The Wave-Outpace-Veto in particular keeps full precedence: it sets bLanePushVetoActive which skips the entire branch block, so the veto's retreat is never overridden. Net effect: this indirectly upgrades the bots' strategic layer — they now perform competitive tower sieges (commit with minion soak, deal damage, retreat on time) that were structurally impossible before, without altering any pre-existing decision.


Dynamic Waypoint System

Instead of always returning to their own base-side tower, bots calculate the frontmost friendly tower on the target lane (not their home lane) and use it as a rally point.
This prevents bots from running home when they should be regrouping at their forward position after a push.

River & Gold Buff Integration

Midlaners opportunistically clear River Buff camps when idle and within 15m.
Carry/Support roles opportunistically clear Gold Buff camps when idle and within 15m.
Buff farming only activates when no enemies are nearby, no defense is needed, and the bot has been idle for ≥2 ticks.

Ward Placement

Mid and Support roles call the native PlaceWard() function when not actively fighting.


Jungler AI
Priority Chain

Defend — Respond to threatened friendly towers (same rules as laners, but jungler only responds to critical structures: inhibitors and core in early/mid, all towers in lategame).
Gank — Score-based target selection (see below).
Lategame Group Push — Join lane pushes when lategame conditions are met or the enemy core is exposed.
Farm — Clear the nearest jungle camp within 80m, or any camp if the jungler is near base (within 60m of core, enabling post-recall farm pickup).
Fallback — Move to nearest friendly tower if nothing else is available.

Gank Scoring System

Each visible enemy hero is scored based on:

Low HP bonus: Up to +50 points for targets at low health percentage.
Overextended bonus: +40 if the target is >50m from their nearest tower, +20 if >30m.
Under tower penalty: −100 if the target is within 20m of a friendly tower (essentially never gank).
Distance penalty: Reduced when the jungler is in base (so post-recall ganks scan the whole map).
Wave timing bonus: +15 if ≥3 friendly minions are near the target (target is likely distracted last-hitting).


Gank/Farm Cycle: Odd match minutes are "gank minutes" (score threshold: 15), even minutes are "farm minutes" (threshold: 45). This creates a natural rhythm of farm-gank-farm without hardcoded rotations.
Tower Dive Logic: If the gank target retreats under their tower, the jungler only continues the chase if the target is below 35% HP.
Gank Commitment: Once a gank is in progress, the jungler follows through even if the score would no longer qualify a new gank — preventing awkward abort-and-return behavior.
45-Second Cooldown Per Target: After a gank ends (success or failure), the same target cannot be ganked again for 45 seconds.

Engage Jump (Synthetic Gap-Closer)

The native Micro Brain does not use a hero's gap-closer/dash ability while ganking — it pursues a kiting target on foot, which lets ranged heroes kite the jungler indefinitely (observed: gank distance crawling 19→18→15→10→3m with no dash). The emulator synthesizes the missing dash: while a gank is engaged and the target is between 3m and 18m away, the jungler receives a physics impulse (LaunchToPoint on the movement component) toward the enemy hero.

Adaptive Distance: The jump lands the jungler ~1m in front of the target (jump = distance − 1m), capped at 6m. It never overshoots past the hero and it closes the 3–7m "dead zone" where a kiter sits just out of melee range; from longer range it covers the full 6m.
Cadence: A 3-second cooldown spaces the hops (~2 m/s sustained, matching native gap-close velocity). The cooldown is only consumed on a real jump (a skipped jump does not spend it) and is refreshed for each new target, so the first hop fires immediately on engage.
Guards: A navigation raycast skips the jump if a wall lies in the path, and a tower guard skips it if the landing point would fall within 18m of a live enemy tower (no tower dives). The jump is suppressed while the jungler is blind or on a heal/recall path, so the escape jump (away) and the engage jump (toward) can never fire against each other in the same tick.
Synthetic by Design: The jump is a physics nudge, not a native ability cast — it carries no ability animation and leaves the K2_SetEnemy target lock untouched, so the native combat AI is never reset. It only changes where the jungler stands, closing the gap so the native Micro Brain can land its own attacks and abilities. (The earlier cast-idle gate was removed after measurement: the combat-ability slot is occupied ~95% of the time during a gank, which is the active combat slot, not an interruptible mid-cast — gating on it suffocated the jump.)

Camp Engagement

Jungle camps are neutral (TeamCreep) and won't aggro until attacked. The emulator handles this by team-flipping the target camp to the enemy team when the bot is within melee range (~3m), which triggers the native Micro Brain's combat response.
The flip is tracked per-bot in FlippedCreep and safely reverted when the bot dies, despawns, changes objective, or finishes the camp.
If an enemy hero appears within 25m while farming, the creep is immediately unflipped and the bot switches to hero combat.


V44 Threat-Sensitive Positioning
Threat Assessment

Every tick, each laner calculates a threat score (sum of nearby enemy hero HP percentages) and a friendly score (own HP percentage + nearby ally HP percentages) within a 30m radius.
If threatScore ≥ friendlyScore × multiplier, the bot enters a threatened state: it stops pushing, backs off toward friendly towers, and the safe-push flag is forced false.
The multiplier scales with game phase: 1.5× in early/mid (more aggressive), 1.2× in lategame (more cautious due to longer death timers).
Heavily Outnumbered Override: If threatScore > 2.5× friendlyScore, laners are forced into threatened state regardless of other conditions.

V44 Movement-Restoration Fix (Phase 3): The original V44-override had a race
condition — it set BotIdleTicks=10 after bIsIdle was already computed earlier
in the tick, so the Nudge system never fired during V44-threatened state and
the bot stayed glued in place. The fix sets bIsIdle=true directly so the
Nudge gate opens immediately, and calls K2_SetEnemy(nullptr) every V44 tick to
break the native Combat-Lock (mirror of the Phase-3 retreat pattern). Two
diagnostic loggers — [V44-MOVE-TRACE] (archived) (periodic 4-hypothesis check) and
[V44-DEATH] (archived) (per-death snapshot during V44=1) — were added to confirm the fix
in match logs.

MIA Phantom Threat System

A simulated vision system tracks when each enemy hero was last "seen" by a friendly hero (within 30m) or friendly tower (within 22m).
When a laner is overextended (closer to the enemy core than their own frontmost tower) and ≥2 enemy heroes have been MIA for 15–120 seconds with their last known position within 50m, a phantom threat of +0.3 per MIA hero is added to the threat score.
This causes overextended bots to retreat when multiple enemies disappear from the map — mimicking the "danger sense" described in the V43 patch notes.


Boss Objectives (Orb Prime & Fangtooth)
Engagement Conditions

Fangtooth: Available after 5 minutes. Requires ≥1 enemy dead. Assigned to Jungler, Carry, Support, and Mid roles.
Orb Prime: Available after 25 minutes. Requires ≥2 enemies dead or ≥1 enemy inhibitor destroyed. Assigned to all roles.
V44 Rule: Boss attempts only occur after a successful group push situation, not opportunistically.

Group Coordination

Bots rally at the boss pit (within 16m arrival radius) and wait for allies before engaging.
Required allies: 3 for Orb Prime, 2 for Fangtooth (counts both bots and human players).
Wait Timeout: If allies don't arrive within 30 seconds, the boss attempt is cancelled and a 30-second team ban timer prevents immediate re-attempts.
Boss Low-HP Finish: If the boss drops below 5% (Orb Prime) or 10% (Fangtooth), the required-allies check is bypassed — the bot finishes the kill.

Two-Phase Combat

Phase 1 (Boss Sleeping): Bots approach with IgnoreEnemies = true (blind mode), preventing them from getting distracted by nearby minions. The first bot to reach melee range triggers the pull with K2_SetEnemy.
Phase 2 (Boss Awake): All bots fight normally. The boss is team-flipped to the enemy team to activate the native combat pipeline.
Pit Invasion: If an enemy hero enters the pit (within ~22m), bots immediately switch to hero combat, unflipping the boss to prevent it from being stolen via team confusion.

Abort Conditions

Defensive Abort: If a friendly inhibitor or core comes under attack, the boss attempt is cancelled (unless the boss is nearly dead — then finish first, except core threats which always override).
Offensive Abort: If the enemy core is now pushable (inhibitor down + enemies dead), bots abandon the boss to end the game.
Kill Tracker: When a boss dies, a global cooldown is applied (7 minutes for Orb Prime, 4 minutes for Fangtooth) during which no new boss assignments are made.
Anti-Ping-Pong: When a boss target is removed (conditions no longer met), a 60-second team ban timer prevents the bots from immediately re-assigning the same boss.
Per-Bot Death Cooldown: If a bot dies during a boss fight, it won't be reassigned to boss duty for 2 minutes after respawning.


Boss Stat Modifications (Debuff via SDK)
Approach

The Boss-Mod block writes directly to Fangtooth's and OrbPrime's GameplayAbility AttributeSets via the SDK, weakening them at runtime. Only the boss pointers (fangtooth, orbPrime) are touched — bot stats are not affected.
Engine resets these attributes at the 120-second scaling tick (and to 0 during boss combat for HealthRegen). The Boss-Mod block runs per bot-tick and re-applies the modifications when it detects an engine-set value.

NOTE (config-driven since the server.json refactor): the parameters below are
NO LONGER constexpr. They are loaded from server.json into the ServerConfig
namespace and re-read on every F1/F4 press (no recompile needed). The launcher
(server_launcher.bat) writes server.json interactively.

Master Switch

enable_boss_mod (bool, server.json key, default true) — gates the ENTIRE
per-tick boss mutation loop. false => boss stays 100% vanilla: HealthRegen,
AttackRating AND MaxHealth are all left untouched (incl. the HealthRegen
reduction). The BOSS-CDO/BOSS-LIVE/BOSS-PERIODIC diagnostics still run
(Lvl-2 gated) so vanilla values stay observable.

Tunable Parameters (ServerConfig, from server.json)

boss_maxhp_mult (= MAXHP_MULT, default 0.9) — Boss MaxHealth multiplier. Lower = less HP = easier kill. Range 0.01..10.0.
boss_attack_mult (= ATTACK_MULT, default 0.85) — Boss AttackRating multiplier. Lower = less damage = bots survive longer. Range 0.01..10.0.
boss_regen_mult (= BossRegenMult, default 0.5) — HealthRegen multiplier applied to each boss's engine base regen (Fang 50, Prime 75). Range 0.01..0.59 (see Rule below). Default 0.5 => Fang 25 / Prime 37.5 (matches the former hardcoded behavior).
boss_stat_epsilon (= STAT_EPSILON, default 0.5) — Detection threshold for "value has changed" checks. NOT a gameplay value. Not exposed in the launcher; only hand-editable in server.json. Don't change unless re-tuning thresholds.
boss_periodic_log_interval_sec (= PERIODIC_LOG_INTERVAL_SEC, default 60.0) — How often to log [BOSS-PERIODIC] snapshot. Cosmetic. Fixed at 60.0 by the launcher.

Per-Boss Parameters (BossSlot array)

Each entry is { ptr, label, baseRegen, regenEngineMin } (baseRegen replaced the
former hardcoded targetRegen — the target is now COMPUTED at runtime):
Fangtooth: { fangtooth, "Fang", 50.0f, 30.0f } — engine base regen 50, engine-min 30
Orb Prime: { orbPrime, "Prime", 75.0f, 50.0f } — engine base regen 75, engine-min 50

Runtime: targetRegen = baseRegen * boss_regen_mult. A clamp then enforces the
Rule below: if (targetRegen >= regenEngineMin) targetRegen = regenEngineMin - 1.0f.

Rule: targetRegen must be < regenEngineMin and < the engine's original value. The regenEngineMin filters out engine combat-zero state (Boss in combat → HealthRegen=0); below this threshold we don't write so the engine's combat behavior is respected. The binding constraint is Fang (50 * mult < 30 => mult < 0.6), hence the 0.01..0.59 config range plus the runtime clamp as defense-in-depth for a hand-edited server.json.

Rotation Intelligence

When a laner has been idle for ≥3 ticks and no enemy minions are within 25m on their own lane, they scan for active fights (allied hero vs enemy hero within ~25m of each other) on adjacent lanes.
If a fight is found within rotation range (~50m) but not too close (~15m, preventing redundant rotations into their own lane fight), the bot rotates to assist.
Rotation Commitment: Once started, a rotation continues for up to 20 seconds or until the target is dead/escaped. The saved target is validated each tick (existence check, HP check, distance check within 60m).
Role Filter: Carry (roleSlot 2) never rotates — they stay on their lane and farm. All other laners can rotate.
Rotations only activate after the first 2 minutes of the match.


Recall & Survival Logic
Health-Based Recall

Sticky Heal System: Below 25% HP, the bot enters "needs heal" state. This persists until HP recovers above 85%, preventing the bot from oscillating between lane and base.
The native BotObjective_Recall with BT_Recall is instantiated and assigned, letting the native recall animation and behavior play out.
Spawn Protection: Heal logic is disabled during the first 30 seconds of the match to prevent false triggers from HP fluctuations at spawn.

Base Emergency Teleport

If a friendly inhibitor or core is under attack and the bot is >100m from its own core, it triggers a tactical recall.
The emergency is validated with a Smart Wave Differential: only triggers if an enemy hero is threatening the base, or if the net enemy minion surplus near the threatened structure is ≥5.
Point of No Return: Bots at the enemy core (or at an enemy inhibitor while their own core is safe) do NOT recall — they finish the game instead.

Turret Escape

Bots detected inside an enemy turret range (K2_IsInsideTurret) without safe-push conditions immediately flee to the nearest friendly tower, even mid-recall.
The blind mode (IgnoreEnemies) is force-cleared during turret escape to prevent the bot from ignoring threats while fleeing.

Boss Override

If a bot needs to recall but is currently fighting a boss below 10% HP, the recall is suppressed until the boss is dead.


Recall-Progress Watchdog (Stuck-Recall Rescue)

Why this exists — the native recall is unreliable on the listen-server. The native recall is a three-layer mechanism: the BotObjective_Recall data object (with BT_Recall and the EQS_TeleportLocation query) decides WHEN to recall, and the engine GameplayAbility UGA_RecallToBase_C performs the channel + teleport. Both the objective-trigger path and the ability activation ultimately depend on the OrionAICommander / BotObjectiveGraph backend, which does not exist on a listen-server. Empirically established (runtime ASC/object probes, an IDA disassembly of BotObjective_Recall.GoldRecall, and ~10 instrumented match logs):
- GoldRecall (the recall-trigger UFunction) unconditionally enqueues a recall command into a BotPlaybook command array that the dead Commander would have drained — by itself it never reaches a working execution.
- The BotObjective_Recall instance carries no execution state (byte-identical between a healthy and a hung recall — verified by a full instance hexdump).
- The Hijack-Firewall is NOT the cause (it never reverts the recall objective during a hang — a dedicated detector fired 0 times over 67 hangs).
- Net behaviour: most recalls still succeed (the bot is near base, or the ability does fire), but intermittently (~1 in 15) a recall hangs — the ability is never activated / channels-but-never-teleports — and the bot stands frozen at its position for 30 s to several minutes.

Why F4's own rescue mechanisms cannot help during a hang (by design): while a bot is in the Recall macro state, F4 deliberately yields full control to the native recall — every tick it sets moveTarget = nullptr and BotIdleTicks = 0, and the Melee Schubser, Anti-Kite and Iron-Rule nudge all explicitly exclude the Recall state. This is correct when the native recall works; it means a hung recall otherwise has zero escape hatch.

Why not a native fix or a predictive detector: the broken layer is the dead Commander / BotObjectiveGraph backend — it cannot be reanimated macro-side. A "predict the stuck early" detector is also inherently impossible: no in-memory signal distinguishes a hung recall from a slow-but-progressing one in real time (the only ground truth is whether a teleport eventually happens — a future event). The only viable, collision-free solution is therefore a macro-side watchdog that detects a non-progressing recall by symptom and walks the bot home itself — exactly the F4 "fill the gap left by the dead navigation" philosophy, identical in spirit to the existing PATH-D / POST-TP-FORCE walk-to-pad mechanisms.

What the watchdog does. It sits inside the recall block (after PATH-D, before PATH-A). Combination trigger: primarily when the recall has shown no teleport AND has been active > 20 s; with a pure-timer fallback at > 35 s (edge safety net). When it fires it drives the bot home with a two-stage MoveToActor — while far it targets the nearest own tower (acceptance radius 1500), and once within ~30 m of that tower it re-targets the HomeBasePad (radius 500); fallbacks cover the no-tower / no-pad cases. Crucially it changes NO macro state — LastMacroAction stays "Recall", the sticky-heal flag stays set, the recall objective pointer is untouched, LastKnownObjective is mirrored so the Hijack-Firewall does not fight it. Because nothing about the Recall state changes, every other subsystem keeps treating the bot as recalling (all the "!= Recall" guards hold), so V44 / Anti-Kite / Lane-Push / Boss / Schubser remain correctly suppressed (verified by a 5-agent collision audit against the actual code: BENIGN). The watchdog self-terminates: the bot reaches the pad, fast-heals to 85 %, the sticky flag clears, the recall ends normally and the bot resumes play.

The teleport-detection signal. A read-only per-tick position-delta probe records, per recall episode, the largest single-tick movement. A real recall teleport produces an instantaneous jump of ~140–200 m in one tick; ordinary movement is ≤ ~7 m per tick. Across instrumented matches this signal is sharply bimodal — a recall either shows ≤ 7 m (never teleported = the hang) or ≥ ~69 m (teleported), with nothing in between — so a "did this recall ever teleport" classification (SawBigJump) is reliable. The primary trigger fires only when no teleport has occurred and > 20 s have elapsed: a healthy recall that teleported by ~10–15 s has SawBigJump set and is therefore never touched, even though the timer was lowered to 20 s. Match logs confirmed zero false interventions on healthy recalls, and confirmed — via cases where the watchdog fired and the bot still teleported afterwards — that the watchdog's walking does NOT cancel a still-channeling native recall.

Honest limitations. The watchdog bounds the damage, it does not cure it: the native teleport for a far bot cannot be restored macro-side, so a genuinely hung far bot still walks home (~50–70 s differential versus a working recall — the inherent cost of the dead backend) — but it is now deterministic and survivable instead of an indefinite freeze. Measurement caveat: the recall-lifecycle "Duration" can be despawn-inflated if a bot dies mid-recall; such cases are marked with a DIED-MID-RECALL event and must be excluded when reading stuck durations.

PATH-D extended to 75 m (own-base scope). The pre-existing "skip the channel and walk to the pad if already near base" optimization (PATH-D) was widened from 50 m to 75 m so it now covers the full friendly inhibitor ring (verified 66–71 m from the pad), not just the core (~27 m). Rationale: at a contested own inhibitor a low-HP bot is safer retreating to the fountain than standing rooted for a ~7 s channel (which is also the unreliable path), and the retreat vector itself points away from the enemy push. Inner/outer towers (≥ ~97 m) stay on the normal channel path. The watchdog's own internal two-stage routing threshold remains 50 m (a different decision: routing granularity of an already-running walk-home, not "skip channel in base").


Recall-Cancel on Proximity (Pinned-Recall Break)

Why this exists — the native recall GameplayAbility UGA_RecallToBase_C roots the bot in place for the duration of its channel (RecallTime), and on a listen-server nothing aborts that channel when an enemy closes in: the abort decision, like the recall trigger itself, was the dead OrionAICommander's job. So a bot that has started channeling and is then dived stands frozen and is killed instead of running. This was confirmed with a read-only probe ([RcAbilActive]) that walks the bot's AbilitySystemComponent and reports whether a UGA_RecallToBase_C spec is actively channeling: the signal is decisive — whenever RcAbilActive=1 the bot's velocity is 0 (rooted), and the two flip together.

What it does. Inside PATH-B (the unsafe-flee branch of the recall block — reached when the bot is in recall state but an enemy hero is too close to safely channel), the emulator locates the live ability instance: AbilitySystemComponent (AOrionChar+0x780) → ActivatableAbilities → the spec whose Ability IsA(UGA_RecallToBase_C) with ActiveCount (+0x28) > 0 → its instance. It clears bDoNotStop (instance+0xBD8) and calls the engine's own K2_CancelAbility on the instance. This releases the channel root, after which PATH-B's flee logic (and the Escape-Jump below) can actually move the bot. It is a pure release — it cancels a native ability the dead backend would otherwise never abort; it does not start, replace, or fake any ability.

Empirical result + safety. Match logs show the cancel firing with ActivePost=0 in the same tick (synchronous teardown), velMs>0 on the following ticks (the bot is moving, no longer rooted), and no oscillation. Reversible: ENABLE_RECALL_CANCEL=false is byte-identical to baseline. Logger [RECALL-CANCEL] (blue-only) reports the teardown; a [RECALL-CANCEL] NO-INSTANCE line covers the rare non-instanced-spec case (a ServerCancelAbility-by-handle fallback would be the next step if it ever appeared).


Synthetic Escape-Jump (Dash/Escape-Ability Replacement)

Why this exists — fleeing heroes in Paragon disengage by repeatedly using their mobility (dash / blink / leap) abilities; a skilled player taps them every time they come off cooldown. The native Epic AI on a listen-server does not do this for the majority of heroes: it fires a hero's escape ability at most once at the start of a flee, and afterwards only when the hero takes further damage — never on cooldown the way a player would. (Like the recall trigger and the turret-detection, the repeated-ability decision was Commander / BotObjectiveGraph-driven, and that backend is gone.) The consequence is that bots flee at run-speed only and are caught by any chaser who still has mobility. The Escape-Jump fills exactly that gap: it gives every fleeing bot a synthetic, repeating mobility hop, restoring parity with what the hero's own kit would otherwise provide — a "Schubser", not a buff (it never exceeds, and is deliberately tuned below, a real dash).

What it does. Inside PATH-B, while the bot is fleeing toward safety, the emulator periodically issues a native OrionCharMovementComponent.LaunchToPoint of 6 m toward the nearest friendly tower. The first hop fires +2 s after the flee begins — this leaves the native escape ability (which does fire once at flee-start) the first move, so the two never stack into an unnatural double-burst — and then every 4 s until the bot is safe. Gates: a friendly tower must exist, the bot must be >12 m from it (otherwise it is already home → PATH-C channel), and no enemy hero may be within 3 m (point-blank stays on the separate PB-LAUNCH, which hops away from the enemy rather than toward the tower). It uses NO K2_SetIgnoreEnemies / Blind — this is explicitly not the old "lockbreak" that froze native movement; the bot keeps fighting and its abilities keep working during the hops. The feature stops itself through PATH-B's own gating (twrD < 12 m, or no enemy hero within ~21 m → the bot transitions to the PATH-C channel). All roles, both teams; logger blue-only; reversible via ENABLE_RECALL_ESCAPE_JUMP.

Wall-check (path-aware, NavigationRaycast). The hop is a ballistic straight line toward the tower, so on a deep flee — where the line to the tower crosses jungle cliffs, river banks or the boss-pit lip — a naive launch would fling the bot into a wall. Before each hop the emulator casts a navmesh ray (Engine.NavigationSystem.NavigationRaycast, Querier = the bot's own AOrionAIBot so the correct nav-agent is used) from the bot to the 6 m endpoint; if the ray stops short of the endpoint there is a wall in the path and the hop is skipped for that tick (the cooldown is not consumed on a skip, so the bot re-checks every tick and hops the instant the direction clears). The first version of this check used K2_ProjectPointToNavigation, which only tests whether the endpoint lands on navmesh and is structurally blind to a wall between the bot and the endpoint — empirically it never caught a single wall (it passed every hop, including ones that visibly hit walls) and was replaced. The gate is sentinel-safe: NavigationRaycast returns the ray start point when no nav-data is available, so a dead API yields "hit at distance ~0", which the gate treats as "do not skip" → the hop fires exactly as before (benign).

Empirical result. The NavigationRaycast is alive on the listen-server (every cleared hop reports the ray reaching the full endpoint, never the dead-API sentinel). In one match the check discriminated cleanly — 9 hops skipped because a wall stood 1.5–6.4 m ahead versus 10 hops fired on a clear path — and the behaviour matched the data: during the skips the bot was provably wall-stuck (distance-to-tower flat or drifting, velocity collapsing toward 0), and on the very tick the direction cleared the hop fired and the bot then progressed home. Honest limitation: it does not, and cannot, save a bot that is already point-blank-pinned at zero velocity by the native combat-lock (the accepted native-combat-lock ceiling) — the hops help a mobile bot on a clear path, not a rooted one.

Tuning (current). Hop distance 6 m, cooldown 4 s, first hop +2 s. The distance was lowered from an earlier 8 m specifically to reduce wall collisions — a shorter ray crosses fewer walls, so more hops pass the path-check (measured wall-skip rate ~47 % → ~26 %, effective hops ~+40 %) — and the shorter 4 s cooldown compensates for the smaller per-hop distance. Net extra retreat speed is ~1.5 m/s (modestly above the 8 m / 6 s it replaced), delivered as frequent small hops, which also reads more naturally than occasional large leaps.


Anti-Kite System (Melee Heroes)

Melee heroes (Offlane, specific Supports like Steel/Narbash/Riktor, and Junglers) that are hitting a minion while an enemy hero is within ~18m and the bot has taken recent damage will switch to chasing the hero.
Activation conditions: not fleeing, not blind, not under enemy turret, HP above 40%, and not in a boss/gank/recall state.
Relaxed distance thresholds when the bot has high HP (>80%), safe push conditions, or no nearby minions — encouraging aggression when it's safe.
Sets HeroFocusQuery as the EQS override to ensure abilities are also directed at the hero, not the minion.

AK Soft-Veto (Phase 2): Before committing to an AK-Charge, the gate evaluates the
local 18m skirmish radius (matches ranged-hero attack range) for two veto conditions:
Wave-Deficit (lpEnM18 > lpFrM18 + 1) and Hero-Outnumber (lpEnH18 > lpFrH18 + 1).
If either condition fires, the AK-Charge is blocked. Exception: a committed melee
exchange at <3m is allowed through, since aborting at point-blank range would
expose the bot's back to free hits. An AK-Collision-Guard ensures the AK-Charge
never overrides an active Lane-Push-Veto (see below).


Melee Schubser (Melee Nudge)

When a bot is in active fighting state but the native Micro Brain has no target (stuck between waves), the Schubser physically moves the bot toward the nearest enemy hero (within 25m) or enemy minion (within 22m) using MoveToActor.
When the native target is a minion but an enemy hero is very close (<8m, or <18m for melee roles with recent damage), the Schubser overrides the target to the hero via K2_SetEnemy.
Tower Safety: The Schubser never pushes bots into enemy turret range and never fires when safe-push conditions favor tower hitting.


EQS Override System

HeroFocusQuery: Forced during ganks and anti-kite scenarios to ensure abilities target heroes, not minions.
JustMinionsQuery: Forced when a laner is in V44 threatened state and far from friendly towers — ensuring the bot focuses on farming defensively rather than engaging heroes.
DragonFocusQuery: Available for boss-related targeting (loaded but currently managed through team-flipping instead).
Overrides are automatically cleared when the triggering condition ends (gank complete, threat resolved, anti-kite disengaged). A safety net ensures stale overrides from HeroFocusQuery or JustMinionsQuery are always cleaned up.


EQS Team Mask Control

The EnemySelectionTeamMask field (offset 0x668) controls which teams the native EQS considers as valid targets.
Base mask: 0x01 (Red) for Blue bots, 0x02 (Blue) for Red bots.
Creep mask (| 0x04): Added for junglers (always), during boss fights, and for laners clearing River/Gold buffs — enabling the native ability pipeline to target neutral creeps.
Mask is reset to base value whenever creep targeting is no longer needed.


Idle Detection & Movement Execution

Idle Tracking: Each bot's position is compared to its last-tick position. If the bot moved less than 1m, an idle counter increments. After 2 ticks idle (4 if enemies are nearby), the bot is considered stuck.
Nudge System: Idle bots receive a MoveToActor call toward their current macro target. The acceptance radius varies by context: 300 (close escort), 600–800 (lane approach), 1500 (base rally), 1800 (tower edge waiting).
Anti-Spam: After each move command, the idle counter is reset to 0 or a negative value (−3 for tight movements), preventing rapid-fire move commands.
The nudge system is explicitly designed as a fallback only — it fills the gap left by the dead ObjectiveGraph's navigation, nothing more.


Iron Rule: Micro vs. Macro Arbitration
The final decision gate that determines whether a bot follows its macro objective or its native micro-brain combat instincts:

Blind Mode (IgnoreEnemies = true): Active during boss rallies, gank approaches, and rotations. The bot ignores all enemies and follows the macro move target. Cleared immediately when the bot arrives at its destination or takes damage.
Native Combat Priority: If the native Micro Brain has a valid target (K2_GetEnemyCharacter returns a living actor) and the bot is not in a "macro says wait" state, combat takes over.
Macro Wait State: When a laner is at an unsafe tower (not safe to push, not fighting, not fleeing), the macro brain suppresses native combat to prevent the bot from chasing enemies into turret range.
V44 Threatened Override: Even during native combat, if the bot is in threatened state and near a friendly tower, it breaks off and retreats.
Damage Tracker: A 2-second combat timer activates whenever the bot takes damage, ensuring it stays in combat mode even during brief gaps between enemy attacks (e.g., between ability cooldowns).


Tower Defense System
Threat Detection

Every friendly tower is scanned for nearby enemies within 22m.
Smart Wave Differential: Instead of triggering on any enemy presence, the system calculates netThreat = enemyMinions - friendlyMinions. Defense only activates when a hero is threatening the tower OR the minion surplus exceeds a threshold (3 normally, 6 for lategame inhibitors).
Hero threats always trigger immediate defense regardless of minion count.

Defender Allocation

Per-tower defender caps prevent overcommitting:

Core: 4–5 defenders
Inhibitor: 3–4 defenders
Inner/Outer: 2 defenders


V44 Lategame Rule: In lategame, bots only defend inhibitors and core — outer/inner towers are sacrificed to maintain push pressure.
V43 Cross-Map Filter: In early/mid game, laners only rotate to defend towers on their own physical lane unless the threatened tower is an inhibitor/core or within 80m.

Defense Prioritization

Higher-tier towers always take priority (Core > Inhibitor > Inner > Outer).
At equal tier, the bot's own lane tower takes priority over cross-lane towers.
At equal tier and equal lane priority, the nearest tower wins.


Inhibitor Collapse-Defense (Blind-Approach Travel, validated 2026-06-01)

WHY THIS EXISTS — the defend decision fired, but the defenders never traveled:
The Tower Defense System above correctly DECIDES who should defend (it sets
bShouldDefend and assigns a WholeLane+BT_DefendTower objective toward the
threatened structure). But empirically the assigned bot did not actually move to
the structure: the Defend branch only wrote moveTarget + accRadius=800 and never
forced movement, while the central MoveToActor nudge fires only when bIsIdle.
A bot that is being shuffled around by native combat-positioning is never idle,
so the cross-lane move never fired — match logs showed defenders sitting 40–110m
from a threatened inhibitor with WouldMove=0, never closing the gap. This made the
intended "collapse from other lanes to defend the inhibitor" behavior effectively
invisible in play. The fix only repairs the EXECUTION (travel) of an already-made
Defend decision; it does not change who is told to defend.

Mechanism — blind-approach travel (mirror of the Boss/Rotation approach):
Inside the laner Defend branch (else if (bShouldDefend)) and the jungler Defend
branch, when the defend target is Tier ≥ 3 (an Inhibitor — bluetower_/redtower_ —
or the Core, classified from a lowercased copy of the target name) AND the bot is
farther than 22m from it (COLLAPSE_TRAVEL_DIST_SQ = 5062500, the same 22m as the
threat-scan radius), the bot is driven home blind: bIsRotatingToObjective = true
(which the Iron Rule turns into BotIsBlind via K2_SetIgnoreEnemies(true), so the
bot ignores en-route distractions exactly like a Boss-rally or rotation approach)
plus bIsIdle = true / BotIdleTicks = 10 so the central nudge actually issues the
MoveToActor toward the structure. On arrival (≤ 22m) the branch sets nothing, so
bIsRotatingToObjective falls back to its per-tick default of false, the Iron Rule
un-blinds automatically, and the bot fights the attackers natively. This reuses
the engine-intended travel machinery rather than fighting the native combat-lock,
and (on a lag-free server) the bot travels at full walk speed (~6.5 m/s).

Anti-transient and anti-stranding timers:
A per-structure persistence timer (g_collapseThreatStart, keyed by the tower
pointer, with a 3s gap-reset that starts a fresh episode if the structure has not
been seen threatened recently) requires the threat to hold for ≥ 2s before any
collapse starts, so a hero briefly clipping the 22m radar does not yank bots across
the map. A per-bot commitment timer (g_collapseStartTime, 22s) keeps a bot that has
started travelling committed to the trip even if the threat momentarily dips,
preventing mid-travel stranding; both maps are erased on the dead-bot and despawn
cleanup paths so a stale timer cannot survive a respawn.

Competitive gates (both make the collapse STRICTLY rarer — they can only suppress a
start, never create new aggressive movement; a committed traveler bypasses them):
Gate A — Reachability cap (COLLAPSE_MAX_DIST_SQ = 144000000 = 120m; corrected
2026-06-12 — the constant had been mis-typed to ~38m, the original design intent was
120m): a bot farther than 120m from the structure does not start a collapse, because
at ~6.5 m/s it could not arrive before the structure (an inhibitor lives ~15–24s
under siege) falls. This blocks the wasted cross-map march (logs showed bots told to
defend from far cross-map who could never have arrived). 120m comfortably covers the
entire base ring (inhibitor-to-inhibitor 34–62m, inhibitor-to-core ~66–71m, mid-fight
positions up to ~108m) plus the borderline band; defenders beyond 120m are instead
handled by Force-Recall (see below), which teleports home rather than walking.
Gate B — Fight-value (cdInWinningFight): the bot counts friendly vs enemy heroes
within 18m (the same FrH18/EnH18 pattern used by the Lane-Push-Veto); if it is in an
active hero fight (CombatTimer > 0, enemy hero < 18m) and not outnumbered
(FrH18 ≥ EnH18), the collapse start is skipped so the bot finishes a winnable
team-fight rather than abandoning it. Minion-only skirmishes and losing fights still
collapse. A Point-of-no-Return term additionally suppresses the collapse while the
bot is itself finishing the enemy Core/Inhibitor under the V45 win-condition.

Collision safety: the whole feature is additive inside the existing Defend branch
and never runs for any other macro action. The blind-approach reuses the same
bIsRotatingToObjective machinery as Boss/Rotation, and every movement-override
subsystem (V44-retreat, Melee-Schubser, all Anti-Kite variants, Blind-Retreat,
Melee-Lockbreak, Lane-Push-Veto, Wave-Outpace, Siege-Push) is structurally
unreachable for a Defend bot (they are Defend-excluded or live in the bestEnemyTower
push branch). The Tier ≥ 3 hero counts are read-only over the per-tick LiveChars
cache, sharing no mutable state. If a collapse is skipped, the bot falls back to the
exact pre-feature Defend state (native fight in place). The truly far case — a
defender so far that even a full-speed blind walk arrives too late and is killed
en route — is NOT handled by this blind-walk collapse; it is now handled by
Force-Recall (see below), which teleports the bot home instead of marching it across
the map. The Tier 1/2 own-lane analog of this collapse is Lane-Return (also below).

Empirical validation (2026-06-01, lag-free match with real inhibitor + core sieges):
27 collapse-travels produced 6 arrivals at threatened structures (BlueTower_1,
BlueTower_0, Core_Blue) across four roles (Offlane, Mid, Carry, Support), including
two bots collapsing onto the same inhibitor and both arriving — the intended
multi-lane gather. Travel ran at full walk speed (a defender closed 72→19m in ~9s),
confirming that the earlier "bots crawl and never arrive" observation was server-lag,
not a design flaw; the defended structures survived (no destruction; Backdoor
protection re-asserted), and no bot died mid-travel. Gate A blocked 7 hopeless
far-marches (96–198m) and Gate B blocked one win-fight reposition. Diagnostic
loggers (all Level 1, not team-gated): [COLLAPSE-DEFEND] per travel tick
(distM/deltaM/blind/persistS/committed/V44), [COLLAPSE-DEFEND] ARRIVE on reaching
the structure, and [COLLAPSE-SKIP] (Reason = Unreachable | WinFight) when a gate
suppresses a start.


Lane-Return (own-lane Outer/Inner defense — Tier 1/2 travel)

WHY THIS EXISTS — the same execution gap as Collapse, but for the cheaper towers:
Collapse-Defense (above) only travels for Tier >= 3 (Inhibitor/Core). A laner whose own
Outer or Inner tower is being pushed had the identical problem — the Defend decision
fired but native combat-positioning kept the bot from actually walking to it. Lane-Return
is the Tier 1/2 mirror of the collapse blind-walk, scoped strictly to the bot's OWN lane.

Scope: laner only (never the jungler), early/mid only (skipped in lategame, where the
V44 rule sacrifices Outer/Inner for push pressure), the threatened tower is Tier 1
(Outer) or Tier 2 (Inner) AND on the bot's own physical lane (gThreatTowerIsMyLane) —
there is no cross-lane pull for the low-value towers (only Collapse pulls cross-lane,
and only for Inhibitor/Core).

Graduated start gates (Outer is treated as more expendable than Inner):
- Hero push: any enemy hero inside the 35m threat radius triggers an immediate return.
- Inner: requires a minion net-threat >= 3 AND the threat to have persisted >= 2.5s.
- Outer: requires persistence >= 4s OR net-threat >= 4 OR the tower already having <= 1
  defender — the strictest/longest gate, since plates are the most sacrificeable.
A team-fight the bot is winning (friendly heroes >= enemy heroes within 18m) is never
abandoned to return (the same fight-value gate as Collapse).

Mechanism: identical blind-walk to Collapse — bIsRotatingToObjective = true (the Iron
Rule turns this into K2_SetIgnoreEnemies(true), so the bot ignores en-route distractions)
plus bIsIdle / BotIdleTicks = 10 so the central nudge actually moves it to the tower,
with a 14s per-bot commitment timer (LANE_RETURN_COMMIT_SEC) so it does not flap.
En-route emergency wakeup: if an enemy hero blocks the path point-blank (< 8m) while the
bot is committed, it un-blinds and fights natively (keeping the commitment) rather than
walking blindly into a point-blank duel.

Exit: arrival (<= 22m) or the start condition no longer holding; the commit timer is
erased on the dead-bot/despawn cleanup paths. Loggers (Level 1): [LANE-RETURN] per travel
tick (reason = HeroPush / MinInner / MinOuter / Commit), [LANE-RETURN] WAKE,
[LANE-RETURN] ARRIVE.


Force-Recall (far-distance defensive reset via native Recall(bool), built 2026-06)

WHY THIS EXISTS — beyond ~90m a blind walk is too slow and too exposed:
Collapse and Lane-Return WALK a defender to the threatened structure. Past roughly 90m
that walk takes too long (the structure falls first) and crosses contested ground blind,
so the bot is focused and killed en route (logs showed defenders dying on cross-map
returns from 120-167m). A high-MMR player in that spot does not walk — they recall,
return to base at full HP, and take the short path back out. Force-Recall reproduces
exactly that: at large distance it triggers a real recall home instead of a blind
cross-map march. It is precisely the "far case" the Collapse section above defers to.

The teleport mechanism (important): it fires the native AOrionAIBot.Recall(bool)
UFunction directly. This matters because the regular recall path (objective injection +
BT_Recall) only drives the native recall channel, whose TELEPORT is HP-gated (roughly
< 25% HP): a full-HP bot that injects a recall objective channels but never teleports —
it hangs. Recall(bool) teleports at ANY HP, which is the entire point — a full-HP far
defender can reset home, which the normal HP-gated recall structurally cannot do.

Scope and gates (the trigger reads the same threat-scan output as Collapse/Lane-Return):
- Distance: > 90m from the threatened structure (FORCE_RECALL_MIN_DIST_SQ), and the bot
  must be farther from its own core than the structure is (genuinely rotated out, not
  just standing in base — this prevents an endless re-fire loop at home).
- Tier split: Inhibitor/Core (Tier 3/4) any lane, even in lategame, up to 2 simultaneous
  force-recallers per structure; Inner (Tier 2) own-lane and early/mid only, max 1; Outer
  (Tier 1) NEVER (the walk back out from base would make it pointless). Junglers are
  eligible only for Tier >= 3.
- Value protection (none of these are pulled home): a bot itself sieging an equal-or-
  higher-value enemy structure (within ~45m), or finishing the enemy Core/Inhibitor under
  the V45 win-condition, or winning a local hero fight (friendly >= enemy heroes in 18m).
- Cap: an isolated per-structure counter binds the simultaneous force-recallers (it does
  NOT write the normal TowerDefenders allocation), so a single threatened inhibitor cannot
  drag the whole team home.

Execution: on a locally-safe tick the recall channel is left to complete (the bot
teleports home, heals, then collapse-walks back to defend); if an enemy is close (hero
< 21m or active damage) the bot instead falls through to the normal flee / recall-cancel
path, exactly like any recall under threat. The episode ends when the bot is home
(< 50m from core), arrives back within 90m of the structure, the threat clears, or an
18s backstop timer expires; the commit/latch state is erased on dead-bot/despawn cleanup.

Honest ceiling: Recall(bool) teleports at any HP, but it still rides the same intermittent
native recall "hang" of the listen-server — the teleport sometimes never fires and the
Recall-Progress Watchdog walks the bot home instead. The hang is most frequent for the
jungler (which recalls most often from deep, contested ground). So Force-Recall reliably
ENABLES the full-HP far reset that nothing else can do, but does not guarantee the
teleport on every attempt. Logger (Level 1): [FORCE-RECALL] FIRE.


Dead Bot & State Cleanup

When a bot dies, all tracking state is reset: positions, idle counters, health, combat timers, macro actions, boss cooldowns, blind mode, heal flags, flipped creeps, rotation targets, and the objective pointer.
FlippedCreep Safety: On death, any team-flipped neutral creep owned by the dying bot is reverted to TeamCreep — but only if no other living bot still needs it flipped (critical for multi-bot boss fights).
EQS Override Cleanup: The override query pointer is nulled on death to prevent stale query references on respawn.
Boss Death Cooldown: If a bot dies during a boss fight, a 2-minute personal cooldown prevents immediate reassignment to boss duty after respawning.

Despawn-Path Migration (F-CRIT-1, verified 2026-05-07): In V45, bots are
destroyed before the engine ticks the cleanup branch that fires when
currentHealth <= 0.0f, making the Health-Path cleanup unreachable in practice.
All cleanups that previously lived on the Health-Path were migrated into the
Despawn-Path (the branch that fires when the bot's pawn becomes null):
BotNeedsHeal=false, BossWaitStartTime=0, BotIsBlind=false +
K2_SetIgnoreEnemies(false), bot+0x0650=nullptr (EQS override),
BossDeathCooldown += 120s, GankCooldownPerTarget += 45s, bot+0x0748=nullptr
(CurrentObjective). Order matters: LastMacroAction must be read before being
reset to "" so Boss/Gank cooldowns can branch on it. The Despawn-Path uses a
WasAlive[bot] transition gate to fire cleanup only once on the alive→dead edge,
not every pre-spawn or respawn-wait tick.



Technical Safeguards

GC-Safe Pointer Validation: All objective pointer writes are validated with IsValidUE4Object and IsA class checks before dereferencing.
Null Guards: Every AllChars, AllTowers, and AllBots iteration includes null checks and Default__ object filtering.
Performance Optimization: The macro tick runs at 1Hz (once per second). Shadow bot management runs at 4Hz. All function pointers are cached at initialization, not looked up per-tick.
BT Safety Net: If an objective's BehaviorTree pointer is ever found null (cleared by the engine or a race condition), it is automatically re-assigned based on the current macro action.
Gank Cooldown Cleanup: Expired cooldown entries are garbage-collected at the start of each tick to prevent unbounded map growth.
MIA Tracker Cleanup: Stale entries (>3 minutes old or referencing despawned actors) are pruned each tick.


Lane-Push-Veto (Phase 5 v5.1, validated 2026-05-07)
The TowerObjective branch (the macro action where laners/junglers commit to
attacking an enemy tower) wraps a 3-mode pre-filter that prevents melee bots
from blindly running into outnumbered or wave-deficit situations. The same
18m skirmish radius used by the AK-Soft-Veto is reused here.

Veto Trigger Conditions (any of):
  Outnumber:      lpEnH18 > lpFrH18 + 1                         (unconditional)
  AloneVsEnemy:   lpFrM18 == 0 && lpEnH18 >= 1                  (unconditional)
  WaveDeficit:    lpEnM18 > lpFrM18 + 1                         (HP < 85% gated)

When a trigger fires, the bot routes to closestFriendlyTower in one of three
modes determined by tower distance and wave support:

  Hold (FrTwrM < 22m):
    accRadius=500, no K2_SetEnemy clear. Bot fights with tower-aggro support.

  ActiveSoft (far from tower, FrM18 >= 1, NOT Hero-Outnumbered):
    accRadius=1500, no K2_SetEnemy clear. Slow-retreat with auto-attacks
    (kite-back) — the bot's abilities continue to work during retreat.

Wave-Outpace-Veto (Hero-less Phase 5 variant, validated 2026-05-14)
The Lane-Push-Veto above requires bestEnemyHero within 18m as its entry gate
(`bIsMeleeChar && bestEnemyHero && closestEnemyHeroDistSq < 3240000.0f`). This
leaves a behavioral gap: when a bot runs ahead of its own minion wave and
fights enemy minions solo without any enemy hero nearby, none of the existing
defensive subsystems detect it — V44 Threat uses a hero-only score (Minions are
filtered out via `if (!cc.bIsHero) continue;`), Phase-5 Lane-Push-Veto needs a
hero in 18m, and AK-Soft-Veto runs only inside the hero-gated AK branch.

Empirical observation from EQS-Dump + match logs confirmed the pattern:
ranged bots (Pyro, Totem, Tacticia, Mambo, Phase, Venus) get pulled forward
by native queries like EQS_OnFrontLine and EQS_AroundLaneGoalInPrimaryAbilityRange
because they want to use their 18-21m attack range — but the own wave is 60m+
behind. SafePush=0 (the native K2_IsSafeToApproachOffensiveLaneGoal returns
false), but the EQS pull is stronger than the macro hint.

The Wave-Outpace-Veto sits directly after the Phase-5 Lane-Push-Veto inside
the second `else if (bestEnemyTower)` of the laner cascade, sharing the same
veto slot (bLanePushVetoActive) so the two are mutually exclusive.

Trigger Conditions (all must hold):
  outer gate:
    !bLanePushVetoActive                                  (Phase-5 has priority)
    bestEnemyMinion && closestEnemyMinionDistSq < 10m²    (bot actively engaged)
    closestFriendlyMinionDistSq > 25m²                    (own wave far away)
    closestFriendlyTower                                  (retreat destination exists)
    !bestEnemyHero || closestEnemyHeroDistSq > 18m        (no hero — else Phase-5)
  inner 18m sweep (reuses the AK-Soft-Veto skirmish radius):
    wovFrM18 == 0 && wovEnM18 >= 2                        (0 own, ≥2 enemy minions)

V45 Win-Condition Exclusion (Push-Finish protection):
  bWovV45WinCondition = (wovDeadEnemyInhibs >= 2)
                      || (wovDeadEnemyInhibs >= 1 && wovDeadEnemies >= 2)
  bWovAtFinishPush    = (bestEnemyTowerTier == 0)
                      && bWovV45WinCondition
                      && (closestEnemyTowerDistSq < 30m)
If bWovAtFinishPush is true, the veto is suppressed — the bot keeps pushing
the enemy core in the V45 win-condition mode. This mirrors the Tactical-Recall
Point-of-No-Return logic (see Recall section) and is strictly tied to the V45
win-condition definition. tier==0 alone is NOT sufficient because the lategame
inhibitor promotion (bLateGame && attackTier==3 → tier=0) also sets it, and a
solo inhibitor push without wave support is not competitive.

Two Modes (no ActiveHard — by design):
  Hold (FrTwrM < 22m):
    accRadius=500, no K2_SetEnemy clear. Bot stays at own tower, fights with
    tower-aggro support.

  ActiveSoft (far from tower, FrTwrM >= 22m):
    accRadius=1500, no K2_SetEnemy clear. Slow-retreat with auto-attacks
    against the chasing minions.

ActiveHard is deliberately omitted: with no heroes present, there is no burst-
damage risk, and a K2_SetEnemy(nullptr) call would tear down the native-AI
auto-attack pipeline against the enemy minions — counter-productive.

Per-bot Persistent State (declared bot-loop scope):
  PrevTickWaveOutpaced[bot]          — flag for despawn-path correlation
  WaveOutpaceVetoLifetimeCnt[bot]    — cumulative veto count (not reset on death)
  LastWaveOutpaceVetoTime[bot]       — timestamp of last veto activation

Addresses the in-combat phase of the retreat/over-extension deaths: once the
native Epic combat AI has "latched on" (enemy hero glued <3–5m), it holds the

Known Limitations (accepted, by design)

Native-AI Combat-Lock at HP=100%: The Macro-Side moveFunc is overridden by
Native-Combat-Positioning when the bot is not actively taking damage.
K2_SetEnemy(nullptr) only breaks this briefly. A permanent override would
require K2_SetIgnoreEnemies, which is rejected as anti-competitive (it makes
the bot blind to threats).

Per-Tick K2_SetEnemy blocks abilities: Empirical knowledge — calling
K2_SetEnemy every tick resets the Native-AI's ability-decision-pipeline so
that no abilities are cast. The codebase's established pattern (Boss, Gank,
and now Phase 5 ActiveHard) uses Smart-Redirect (only call when the target
differs) or Transition-Trigger (1× per episode with a 2s threshold).

Native API dead on Listen-Server (Phase 4 finding):
GetCurrentLaneFrontWaveTeamProgress, GetCallForHelpRating, and
GetRecentDamageTakenFromActor return FLT_MAX / 0 / 0 respectively. These
APIs depend on the BotObjectiveGraph / AICommander backend that does not
exist on a Listen-Server, and reanimating them would require deep engine-
level reverse engineering.

Boss-Pit Deaths (~37% of total deaths): BY DESIGN. The boss is intentionally
very strong, and bots die either to the boss itself or to enemy team
contests at the pit. This is not a bug and is out of scope for retreat-logic
optimization.

Defend-Action without Veto-Coverage: Phase 5 v5.1 covers the TowerObjective
branch but not the Defend-Action branch (L. 9061-9607 in dllmain.cpp).
1-4 Defend-deaths per match still occur in 1v2 outnumber situations at
threatened towers. This is tracked as a planned Phase 6.5. The Phase 6.5 design must include a Tower-Tier-Gate so that
Inhibitor (Tier 3) and Core (Tier 4) defenses never retreat (the bot
sacrifices for critical structures), while Outer (1) and Inner (2)
defenses use the same 3-mode veto as Phase 5.


Bot Buff Routing System (Phase 7, validated 2026-05-10)
The native engine ships with a per-team buff routing mechanism that the F4
Macro Brain extends and tunes. The system has three layers:

Layer 1 — GameplayEffect Routing (Engine-side, native)
The engine applies one of two GameplayEffects to every spawned hero bot at
match start, automatically routed by the bot's relationship to the human
player on the listen-server:
  GE_PVE_BotBuff_Friendly_C  -> bots in the human's team (allies)
  GE_PVE_BotBuff_Enemy_C     -> bots in the opposing team (enemies)
Verified empirically via a per-bot ActiveGameplayEffects walker
([BUFF-APPLY] (archived) tag) that iterates UAbilitySystemComponent.ActiveGameplayEffects
@ ASC+0xCA0 and checks each FActiveGameplayEffect.Spec.Def class name. When
the human switches teams between matches, the GE assignment flips correctly
— this is the engine's own Per-Team-Detection, not a custom mechanism.

Layer 2 — CurveTable-Driven Magnitudes (Engine-side, native)
The two GameplayEffects pull their modifier magnitudes from
UOrionGameData::DifficultySettingsTable (a UCurveTable* @ +0x50). Two
mirrored sets of rows define the per-attribute scaling:
  Bot.Buff.{BasePower,Health,HealthRegen,ManaRegen,Gold}           — ENEMY
  Bot.Friendly.Buff.{BasePower,Health,HealthRegen,ManaRegen,Gold}  — FRIENDLY
Each row is a FRichCurve with 30 keys (Time-axis = hero level 1..30,
Value-axis = additive bonus). Verified via direct memory walk of the
RowMap @ UCurveTable+0x30 (TMap<FName, FRealCurve*>) plus VTable RTTI
disassembly in IDA which identified the FIndexedCurve class hierarchy.
FRichCurveKey layout: 28 bytes (Time @ +0x04, Value @ +0x08, six floats
+ three 1-byte enums + 1 byte padding, no trailing alignment padding).

Layer 3 — Per-Team Multiplier Patch (F4 Macro Brain extension)
Two configurable multipliers in the F4-init block patch the CurveTable
in-place at server startup:
  ENEMY_BUFF_MULTIPLIER     (default 2.0f) — multiplies all Bot.Buff.* keys
  FRIENDLY_BUFF_MULTIPLIER  (default 2.0f) — multiplies all Bot.Friendly.Buff.* keys
The patch walks UCurveTable.RowMap, finds the 10 target rows by FName
(via Engine's KismetStringLibrary::Conv_StringToName), and multiplies every
keyframe's Value field by the configured factor. Memory-write only, no
GameplayEffect re-application — the engine reads the new values when GEs
are applied at bot spawn. Master switch ENABLE_BUFF_PATCH=true gates the
entire patch action; buffPatchAlreadyApplied static prevents compounding
across multiple matches in the same server session (otherwise 2x would
become 4x on the second match, etc.).

Verification (2026-05-10): With ENEMY_BUFF_MULTIPLIER=FRIENDLY_BUFF_MULTIPLIER=2.0,
POST-EVAL via UDataTableFunctionLibrary::EvaluateCurveTableRow confirms all
30 keys per row exactly doubled compared to the [BUFF-VERIFY] baseline:
  Bot.Buff.BasePower    @T4: 4 -> 8     @T5: 8 -> 16    @T6: 27 -> 54
  Bot.Buff.Health       @T4: 0.25 -> 0.5
  Bot.Friendly.Buff.Health @T4: 0.5 -> 1.0
The Direct-Memory-Read sample logs and the EvaluateCurveTableRow POST values
match perfectly — the engine sees the patched values.

Conflict Isolation (verified 2026-05-10)
The buff patch operates exclusively on FRichCurveKey.Value floats inside
the UCurveTable's RowMap. Zero overlap with established Macro Brain state:
  - Hijack-Firewall (bot+0x0748): no contact (different memory region)
  - StaticConstruct (UObject creation): no contact (no new UObjects spawned)
  - Backlink (existingObj+0x0128 = CommanderObj): no contact (CurveTable is
    a separate UDataAsset, not a BotObjective)
  - F4 Macro Brain tick loop: no contact (CurveTable is read-only at runtime
    by the GE-magnitude evaluator, the Macro Brain never touches it)
  - F1 Spawn logic: orthogonal (CurveTable is read at GE-application-time,
    after F1 has already finished spawning)

Known Limitations / By-Design
  - The CurveTable is one shared resource — there is no native mechanism
    for per-bot or per-role buff variation beyond the Friendly/Enemy split.
    Per-role melee-bot tuning would require additional per-bot ASC mods
    (out of scope as of Phase 7).
  - The Time-axis on the curves is hero level (1..30), not difficulty stage.
    EvaluateCurveTableRow with InXY=4 returns the bonus at level 4, not
    at "Veteran". The actual stage-based selection happens via the
    BotDifficultyLevelIndication indicator and a separate
    FAIBotDifficultySettings array (UOrionAISettings@0x0F8) that the engine
    consults for BEHAVIOR (aim, recall, threat thresholds), separate from
    STAT BUFFS (the curve table).
  - The multiplier-compounding protection is per-server-session only. An EXE restart
    reloads the .uasset fresh, so the patch starts from defaults again.
  - Minion.* and Objective.* curves are all 1.0 in the loaded .uasset
    (no Epic-defined difficulty scaling for those). Patching them would
    work but would have global effect (no per-team distinction for
    minions/towers).

Deep-Over-Extension — Influence-Map prevention lever: INVESTIGATED + CLOSED (2026-05-27)
The dominant residual death is Deep-Over-Extension: a melee/ranged bot pushes 73-162m ahead of its
own tower, an enemy commits/chases, the retreat fails. As a prevention lever
the native Influence-Map system was reverse-engineered via IDA and three uses were checked
— ALL unsuccessful:
  Verified offsets/subs: GetInfluenceValueAt (Read, grid accessor sub_14043CD90),
  UpdateInfluenceSurce (Write, -> OrionAIInfluenceMapManager::AddInfluence sub_140448290),
  Manager on UOrionAISystem@0x138, UOrionQueryTest_InfluenceMap::RunTest (sub_14041D900) =
  the one real grid reader (the EQS-QueryTest scores candidate locations).
  (1) infVal as a READ discriminator in the push decision: live but broad/dead —
      it does not separate lethal-deep from safe-deep (measured, discarded).
  (2) Influence INJECTION as prevention (strengthen the enemy source so the native EQS avoids
      deeper): closed. The deep push is macro-commanded (explicit MoveTo, not overridable by
      EQS); the write is actor-anchored + positive-only + (Team,Lane)-global (no
      per-bot targeting); and prevention hits the same wall.
  (3) Influence injection REACTIVE (after commit, execution/disengage help): closed.
      Combat micro is BehaviorTree/AbilityComponent/Focus-driven, not Influence-EQS;
      on our own side we already intervene maximally (BlindRetreat = blind+SetEnemy-Clear+Launch:
      37m displacement, yet net -13m deeper, the pursuer glued); and the bias-able window
      (pursuer far/EQS-positioned) is disjoint from the lethal window (pursuer
      point-blank/combat-locked).
Unifying reason: the outcome depends on the enemy commit decision — fundamentally
unpredictable BEFORE the push (empirically confirmed over 72 VIS-DISC episodes: no static
signal separates them; counter-pair Mambo 38%HP/3v0/Hero@3m SURVIVES vs Ice 100%HP/no-hero/85m DIES),
and AFTER the commit the enemy is combat-locked and thus not reachable by bias/EQS injection.
The RE knowledge stays documented; as an actionable lever for Deep-Over-Extension it is FINALLY
CLOSED. Residual = retreat execution + enemy-commit ceiling (accepted by design, analogous to
Boss-Pit deaths = good enemy play, not a bot bug).

Solo-1v1 Veto (SOLO1V1-VETO, armed 2026-07-17)
The use case: a lone melee laner (typically Sword) trades against a ranged hero (Medic) who stands inside his own minion wave. The melee bot loses the exchange, the native combat lock keeps it in place and it dies. Before this subsystem nothing reacted to a measurably lost 1v1 trade - V44 scores heroes only by HP ratio and misses a 1v1 with a minion deficit. SOLO1V1 is a reactive blind disengage that reuses the proven Lane-Return blind-approach pattern.

Trigger Conditions (all must hold, dllmain.cpp:11363-11377):
  ENABLE_SOLO1V1_VETO && bIsMeleeChar && bestEnemyHero
  !(ENABLE_Q3_SV_NO_JUNGLER && bIsJungler)          (Q3: never for junglers)
  closestEnemyHeroDistSq < 18m                      (hero in contact)
  akvFrH18 == 0                                     (alone, no allied hero in 18m)
  closestFriendlyTower                              (retreat destination exists)
  !bV44Threatened, or the V44-Dive exception below
  !bMeleeTowerHold                                  (own-tower zone belongs to MTH / V44-HOLD)
  !bAkDeathZoneVeto || ENABLE_SOLO1V1_DEATHBALL      (true -> deathball lanes are covered)
  !bAkKiteback
  LastMacroAction not Boss/Gank/Recall/Rotate; Defend only if bP8FarDefend (Paket 8 K-3, see the Paket-8 overview)
and one of two fire reasons:
  svLosing:        (botDrop - heroDrop) >= SOLO1V1_LOSE_THRESH (15 %HP) within the current episode; the episode HP anchors are re-initialised after > 8 s without passing the gate (:11391-11393).
  svShieldedDive:  ENABLE_SHIELDED_DIVE: !bIsJungler, !bLanePushVetoActive, hero >= 3m (no committed melee), akvEnM18 - akvFrM18 >= SHIELDED_DIVE_DEFICIT (2) and >= SHIELDED_DIVE_MIN (1) enemy minion within 14m of the HERO (:11398-11419). This is the positional reason: it fires without any trade loss. The original code comment (removed in the release cleanup) recorded the measured basis (debugger5, Sword): taken/dealt ratio 1.33 with hero <= 18m, 7.24 once the hero is shielded by minions, 14.11 with minion deficit; at that stage the bot deals only 0.19 %HP/s, so disengaging sacrifices nothing.
A V45 core-finish exclusion (svFinish, mirror of lpAtFinishPush) suppresses the fire on the enemy core during the win condition.

Variants inside the gate:
  ENABLE_SOLO1V1_V44DIVE: SV may fire although V44=1 when akvEnM18 > akvFrM18, the enemy hero is ranged (bAkHeroIsRanged = hero not in the melee name list) and the own tower is > 16m away (TOWER_SAFE_SQ). This is the window in which the V44 retreat is structurally dead; it superseded ENABLE_V44_STUCK_BREAK (false).
  ENABLE_H1B_SV_ADV (H1b): the shielded-dive reason is dropped when the bot is not losing the trade and the shared advantage helper H1AdvEnter() says the bot is ahead (thresholds: see part 2, H1 / ADV-WIDE). svLosing itself is unchanged. Logger [SV-ADV-SKIP] (archived).

Action (dllmain.cpp:11431-11468):
  K2_SetIgnoreEnemies(true), BotIsBlind=true             (blind is the lock-break; NO K2_SetEnemy call)
  moveTarget = latched retreat target (see next section)
  accRadius  = 500 (ENABLE_SOLO1V1_WAVEHOLD; 1500 without) - deep at the tower instead of the 15m poke edge
  bIsActivelyFighting=false, bIsIdle=true, BotIdleTicks=10
  g_svActiveUntil = now + SOLO1V1_WINDOW_SEC (5 s); episode start time and HP stamped on the first FIRE only
  ENABLE_SOLO1V1_STICKY: stamps lastThreatRetreatTime (2.5 s sticky, like LPV-Hard / V44 / AK-DZ); inside the sticky localFrontMinion is nulled (:8380) - its only readers are the MELEE-FRONTPUSH sites (:9912 / :10364), so the extra effect is melee-only.

Collisions / priority: SV fires before the AK gate; the blind automatically suppresses AK-Charge and the Schubser (both !BotIsBlind-gated). WAVE-FALLBACK cannot fire while an SV window runs (SV has priority); BR-WAKE yields to an SV window (ENABLE_BRWAKE_SV_YIELD); AK-DZ-ESCAPE defers to it (:11963). In the own-tower zone (< 16m) MELEE-TOWER-HOLD owns the bot; the V44-Dive term uses the exact complement (> 16m), which is why both were moved together in C6. Open findings: the SV gate does not read bLanePushVetoActive (only the shielded-dive term does), so LPV and SV can fire in the same tick (6/22 and 7/25 in two human-Medic logs); and the trade anchor g_svEpHeroHP is not bound to the hero identity - a switch of bestEnemyHero looks like "hero +54 HP" and can create svLosing at parity (4 of 13 svLosing fires in those logs, all Chains). Both are still present in the current code; harm beyond those counts is unverified.

Empirical validation:
  debugger7 (2052 s): 10 FIRE, about 8/10 extracted incl. point-blank (Sword T=593: heroM=1, pulled home 67->30m), 0 % false suppression; residual ~2/10 hard-glue deaths.
  Later finding: SV bounced at the own tower (FIRE -> retreat -> CLEAR atTower -> re-push, 3 cycles of ~7 s) because the macro action stays TowerObjective - fixed by routing that zone into V44-HOLD (MELEE-TOWER-HOLD, below).
  SV owns only 1.7 % of the melee contact ticks (15/867); Q3 confirmed (0 SV lines for junglers).
SOLO1V1 is the retreat owner of the use case and stays armed. It is reactive by design: the predictive minion-deficit trigger was measured and refuted (recall 2/5 vs 5/5 for the reactive veto).


SOLO1V1 Retreat Target and Release Chain (Paket 4, Paket 8)
The first SOLO1V1 builds released too early (39 of 45 CLEARs in the FIRE tick itself, median tower distance at CLEAR 65m) and ping-ponged between targets. A chain of small switches now governs WHERE the bot retreats to and WHEN the window ends.

Retreat target - svPickTarget (dllmain.cpp:11348-11362) with ENABLE_SV_TARGET_LATCH (Paket 4 K-A):
  1. own wave anchor (rearWaveAnchor) if > 15m away, closer than the own tower, and with substance: own lane minions within 8m of the anchor >= akvEnM18 - 1 (svWaveSubstanceOk, :11329-11338);
  2. otherwise the own tower if <= 60m (WFB_TOWER_MAX_SQ);
  3. otherwise nullptr = NOTARGET: no retreat at all.
  The target is captured at FIRE and held while it is alive and, for a wave target, while that minion is still behind the bot (svTgtAlive :11339; passed-check in MAINTAIN :12863-12890); only then it is re-picked. The latch removes target ping-pong. Side effect: it overwrites the older DEATHBALL target writes (:11441 is overwritten at :11443; the MAINTAIN else-branch :12972-12981 is practically unreachable while the latch is on) and it requires ENABLE_SD_DEEPANCHOR=false.
  Known limitation (by design, measured): an own wave closer than 15m is never chosen - the 15m threshold avoids the accR dead zone, so the bot runs to the tower instead (12 of 13 melee decisions went to the tower, 5 of them with a substantial wave 1-5m away). Changing it was audited and refuted twice (Entwurf E: 2 new blind episodes / 0 events / <= 2 of 5 effective; T-2).

Release chain (SV-MAINTAIN, dllmain.cpp:12829-12983), evaluated every tick while the window is armed:
  waveOK (ENABLE_SOLO1V1_WAVEHOLD): own minions in 18m >= enemy minions -> release and push WITH the wave.
  ENABLE_SOLO1V1_HOLDFIX: parity needs at least one own minion (0 vs 0 is not parity) and is honoured only after >= 1 s of holding (SOLO1V1_MIN_HOLD_SEC). Fixed the same-tick collapse (0 of 4 CLEARs in the FIRE tick, vs 39 of 45 before).
  ENABLE_SV_RELEASE_HP_V2 (Paket 4 K-C): parity release only at HP >= 50 % OR own wave >= enemy wave + 2 (:12907-12908).
  ENABLE_RETREAT_COVER_RELEASE (Paket 4 K-B): parity counts only "in cover" = own tower < 18m OR (>= 2 own minions in 18m, not outnumbered, and the wave stands ahead of the bot (rwAheadM25 >= 1) or the anchor is within 8m) (:12767-12769). While not in cover the window is extended in 1.5 s steps up to a hard cap of 14 s after FIRE (RETREAT_MAX_HOLD_S); a maxHold CLEAR arms a 2 s re-FIRE lock (g_svMaxHoldT, read at :11420). "safe" (hero > 25m) also requires cover. MAINTAIN re-stamps the sticky.
  ENABLE_P8_SV_POS_RELEASE (Paket 8): parity is ignored when no own minion stands ahead of the bot (rwAheadM25 == 0) during the first 4 s after FIRE or while the hero is <= 20m (not for jungler/boss/lategame). Ledger: armed, never causally triggered so far.
  towerHold: own tower < 16m, hero < 20m, solo, enemy minions > own + 1 -> release so that MELEE-TOWER-HOLD / V44-HOLD takes the bot next tick (fight at the tower instead of standing blind).
  arrivedOk: within 8m of a wave target with FrM18 >= EnM18 (arriving in deficit keeps holding). tgtGone: no target left. timeout: window expired.
  Handoff: rotation, Boss/Gank/Recall/Rotate, or Defend without far-defend -> window dropped WITHOUT touching the blind (the new state / Iron Rule owns it).
  ENABLE_SOLO1V1_ANCHORRESET: on CLEAR, if the bot lost no HP since FIRE, the episode HP anchors are erased - ends the blind flicker caused by a stale ("latched") trade anchor.
  On CLEAR the engine is un-blinded only if BR/WFB do not still own the blind.

Empirical: Paket 4 "works as built, no measurable benefit"; Arm Z: 7 of 14 retreat episodes still ended without cover, mostly through other owners (BR safe, escort pull-back under V44, WFB arrived without parity, LPV drop) - this led to P23-COMMIT. Wave-Fallback (WAVE-FALLBACK, armed 2026-09-06) + Deficit-Extension, DZ-Inhib, H1c / A'
WAVE-FALLBACK (WFB) closes the "no own wave" half of the use case: a lone melee laner with NO own minion within 18m, an enemy minion surplus of >= 2 and an enemy hero within 18m. In that state the own tower is the wrong anchor: it is usually far away, and the walk crosses the tower edge band 17-22m, which was measured as the most expensive zone. WFB therefore retreats blind to the nearest own lane minion BEHIND the bot and uses the tower only as a nearby fallback.

Trigger Conditions (all must hold, dllmain.cpp:11608-11626):
  ENABLE_WAVE_FALLBACK && bIsMeleeChar && !bIsJungler && bestEnemyHero
  !h1WfbAdv                                         (H1c, see below)
  closestEnemyHeroDistSq < 18m
  hero >= 4m (WFB_WAKE_HERO_SQ), or wfbPbHeavy       (point-blank only in heavy deficit, Paket 3)
  akvFrH18 == 0                                     (alone)
  akvFrM18 == 0, or wfbDefCand                      (no own minions, or the deficit extension)
  akvEnM18 - akvFrM18 >= 2                          (enemy minion surplus)
  akvFrM18 == 0 || !bLanePushVetoActive || wfbLpStalled   (deficit path defers to a moving LPV retreat)
  !(bMeleeTowerHold && own tower < 17m)             (the 17-22m edge band is WFB territory, < 17m is MTH)
  no active SOLO1V1 window (SV has priority), !bAkKiteback, no WFB window already running
  LastMacroAction not Boss/Gank/Recall/Rotate; Defend only with bP8FarDefend AND akvFrM18 == 0 (Paket 8 K-3)
  not in the V45 core finish (wfbFinish)

Target choice (dllmain.cpp:11633-11698):
  Candidates: own lane minions (bp_minion_lane_) strictly behind the bot (farther from the enemy core), 8-70m from the bot (WFB_WAVE_MIN_SQ / WFB_WAVE_MAX_SQ), on the bot's own physical lane (Voronoi test against the front friendly towers of the other two lanes); the nearest wins.
  Priority: towerPrio if the tower is <= 30m (SOLO1V1_TOWER_PRIO_SQ) -> wave if closer than the tower -> tower if <= 60m (WFB_TOWER_MAX_SQ). No target -> no fire.
Action: K2_SetIgnoreEnemies(true), BotIsBlind, moveTarget = target, accRadius = 500, bIsActivelyFighting=false, bIsIdle=true / BotIdleTicks=10, commit window g_wfbUntil = now + 14 s (WFB_COMMIT_SEC = LANE_RETURN_COMMIT_SEC), sticky stamp, engine-blind tracker g_wfbEngineBlind (BLOCKER fix: the flag must mirror the native call).
MAINTAIN / CLEAR (dllmain.cpp:12985ff): tgtGone (target dead), arrived (< 8m), safe (hero > 25m AND in cover, Retreat-Cover-Release), stall (4 ticks with < 4m net progress, WFB_STALL_*), timeout (14 s), parity (deficit episodes only, see below). WAKE: hero < 4m -> un-blind and fight natively (never in deficit episodes). Handoff as in SOLO1V1, plus "an SV window armed later takes over deficit episodes" (wfbHandoffSV). The blind is returned only if BR/SV do not still own it.

ENABLE_WFB_DEFICIT_EXT (Paket 3): extends WFB to the "remnant wave" case: akvFrM18 >= 1 with a deficit >= 2, Tier 1/2 enemy tower (wfbDefCand, :11477-11479). In this path the wave candidate needs substance (own lane minions within 8m of it >= akvEnM18 - 1, WFB_DEF_SUBST_R_SQ), the tower anchor is the front friendly tower of the own lane and must be behind the bot; the episode ends at real parity (FrM18 >= 1 and >= EnM18 for 2 consecutive ticks) instead of at the counting edge; no WAKE inside a deficit episode, and BR-WAKE is suppressed during it too (brInWfbDef, :12037). wfbPbHeavy (heavy deficit >= +3 with <= 1 own minion, hero point-blank) lets the episode start even at < 4m.
ENABLE_WFB_DZ_INHIB (Paket 4 K-D): the deficit path also at the enemy inhibitor (Tier 3 or lategame-promoted 0, never the core, enemy structure < 30m, <= 1 own lane minion in 25m, heavy deficit). Armed but never reached in d12-d16.
ENABLE_H1C_WFB_ADV (H1c): no WFB entry while the bot is clearly ahead (H1AdvEnter on bot/hero HP, no hero outnumber, not in heavy deficit). Logger [WFB-ADV-SKIP] (archived). Required by audit KO: without it WFB would take over exactly the cases that H1b/H2 hand back to the fight.

Trigger case d17 T=459 (Sword 66 -> 12 % while WFB was blocked by H1c). Expected ~0.2 activations per match; logged as MC wfbMiss=h1c and [WFB-WHYNOT] why=h1c. Lane-Return WAKE Guard (LR_WAKE_S0_GUARD)
Lane-Return (documented above) has an en-route emergency wake: if a hero blocks the path point-blank (< 8m) while the bot is committed, it un-blinds, nulls moveTarget and fights natively. For a lone melee bot in a minion deficit this wake starts exactly the point-blank fight the use case wants to avoid ("damage moves to the LR-WAKE edge").
ENABLE_LR_WAKE_S0_GUARD (dllmain.cpp:9173-9232, armed): an "S0" predicate is computed locally for melee laners with a hero < 18m: no allied hero in 18m AND enemy minions > own minions + 1 (lrS0Raw). It is latched per lane-return episode (g_lrS0Latch, set on the blind-walk tick, erased with the commit anchor). While raw or latched S0 holds, the WAKE is suppressed (lrWakeFight = lrFar && lrCommit && lrHeroBlocking && !lrS0Guard) and the blind walk continues. Logger [LANE-RETURN] WAKE-GUARD (2 s throttled).
Target group: the guard is melee-only (lrS0Raw is only computed under bIsMeleeChar); Lane-Return itself runs for ALL laners (!bIsJungler && !bLateGame, Tier 1/2, own lane). Consequence (code fact, effect unmeasured): a RANGED laner on a lane-return walk is still woken at hero < 8m and told to fight point-blank, the worst distance for a 15-18m attack range.
Status: armed; latch without time limit (B3, 0 occurrences). The stop-threshold variant ENABLE_LR_S0_COVER (stop at 16m instead of 22m in the S0 case) is false. Lane-Return itself is classified as rotation/own-lane-defend logic and is deliberately not extended (project rule).

Offensive / Positioning Family since beta9 (Part 2 of the additions since beta9)
This part documents the logic added or changed since `beta9-escape+retreatjump-fix` that decides WHERE a laner stands relative to its own wave and WHEN it is allowed to commit onto an enemy hero: the Wave-Ahead / Hero-Beyond-Front position vetoes, the Schubser discipline guard, the opportunity / kill windows (F2, I2, K2), the advantage hold (H1 / ADV-HOLD / K3), the kill-secure exception of the Lane-Push-Veto (H2), MELEE-WAVE-HOLD with its escort-cascade insertion, the Death-Zone HeroFocus gate in Defend, the measured 16m tower safety zone and the trade tracker that several of these read. Retreat / Defend logic is in Part 1, Jungler / global / logger topics in Part 3.

Line numbers refer to the published Server/dllmain.cpp (14,716 lines) and shift with later edits; grep the switch or logger name if a number no longer fits. Unless stated otherwise every switch below is a `static const bool` where `false` restores the previous behavior byte-identically.

Common ground (applies to almost every section below):
  Melee classification is the name list `bIsMeleeChar` (dllmain.cpp:5079-5097, unchanged since beta9; note that Femme/Countess is in the MELEE list). Nearly all new offensive/positioning blocks are gated `bIsMeleeChar && !bIsJungler` — ranged laners and junglers are excluded unless a section says otherwise. Exceptions that DO reach ranged bots are called out explicitly (Schubser Guard A1, DZ-HeroFocus Defend gate, tower safety zone A2).
  The 18m skirmish counters `akvFrM18 / akvEnM18 / akvFrH18 / akvEnH18` (dllmain.cpp:10815-10830) are computed for ALL bots without a class gate, so `bAkWaveDeficit`, `bAkOutnumber`, `bAkHeavyDeficitNoBacking` and `bAkDeathZoneVeto` (dllmain.cpp:10831-10832/11058-11059) are live for ranged bots too.
  Macro tick is ~1 Hz; every actuator below either persists through a window/latch or is re-written each tick. The native Objective subtree (BT_AttackTower / BT_DefendTower) is NOT the forward motor: the BlackBoard ObjectiveInstance is null on the listen-server (126/126); forward movement in the lane is mostly native minion pursuit (57-74 % of forward meters). The writes below therefore act on moveTarget / Nudge, K2_SetEnemy and the EQS slot 0x0650, not on the objective BT.


AK Wave-Ahead Veto + Hero-Beyond-Front (position terms of the AK-Soft-Veto, active)
The AK-Soft-Veto of Phase 2 only counted heads (Wave-Deficit, Hero-Outnumber inside 18m). It had no notion of WHERE the bot stands relative to its own wave. Measurement over five logs showed that the expensive state is "bot in front of its own wave tip" (no own minion closer to the enemy tower than the bot): 0.71-1.11 %HP/s against 0.19-0.36 %HP/s as soon as one own minion is ahead of him, a 3-5x jump that does not depend on the exact distance. A later A/B pair replicated the sign of this `waveLeadM` cost gradient in 8/8 sub-cuts (Arm P +1.29 %HP/s, Arm Q +1.24 %HP/s); only the sign is robust, the gradient is not monotonic. Two position terms were therefore added to the existing veto machinery.

(1) Wave-Ahead term (ENABLE_AK_WAVE_AHEAD_VETO, dllmain.cpp:3816, computed dllmain.cpp:11066-11097):
Inline 25m sweep over own lane minions (AK_WAVE_AHEAD_R_SQ = 25m, same definition as the [WAVE-FRONT-PROBE] (archived) logger). bAkWaveAhead = (own minions within 25m >= 1) AND (none of them is closer to the enemy tower than the bot). Kill exception: if the hero is <= 30 % HP (AK_WAVE_AHEAD_KILL_HP) the term is cleared — a dying hero is not let go. The term only extends the existing disjunction (dllmain.cpp:11138):
  bAkSoftVeto = (bAkWaveDeficit || bAkOutnumber || bAkWaveAhead) && !bAkCommittedPunishOK
so the unchanged committed-melee exception (hero < 3m, no heavy deficit) still lets a started melee exchange continue. This extension of `bAkSoftVeto` is the only actuator-relevant change of all changes since beta9 without its own switch in the gate chain (U4 A6); the new term itself is switch- and melee-gated.

(2) Hero-Beyond-Front (ENABLE_AK_HERO_BEYOND_FRONT, dllmain.cpp:3819, computed dllmain.cpp:11098-11136, Paket 1):
Covers the parity case that Wave-Deficit cannot see ("damage falls at parity"): the enemy hero stands closer to the enemy tower than the bot's own front-most minion, i.e. beyond the own wave tip, so chasing him means leaving the wave.
Trigger Conditions (all must hold, base term `ahbfBaseT`):
  enemy hero < 18m and NOT committed melee (>= 3m)
  akvFrH18 == 0                                   (alone, no allied hero)
  akvFrM18 < akvEnM18 + 2                         (own wave not clearly superior)
  own minions within 25m >= 1                     (otherwise Wave-Deficit owns it)
  bestEnemyTowerTier == 1 or 2                    (never inhibitor / core / lategame promotion)
  position: heroTwrM < frontMinionTwrM - AHBF_ENTER_M (0m) on entry, < frontMinionTwrM + AHBF_EXIT_M (2m) to stay
  hero HP > 45 % on entry, > 40 % to stay (AHBF_KILL_HP 40)
A 2s minimum hold (AHBF_MIN_HOLD_S) bridges short minion-count / tier flips; the episode start is stored per bot (g_ahbfOnT, erased on dead/despawn/recall).
Consumers of bAkHeroBeyondFront: the classic AK-Charge gate (dllmain.cpp:11915, `&& !bAkHeroBeyondFront`; AK-JUMP hangs on bAntiKiteActive and is suppressed with it), both hero writes of the Schubser (dllmain.cpp:12451 / 12496: no moveTarget=hero, no K2_SetEnemy retarget — the native target, usually a minion, is kept), and F2/AK-OPP (only in TO mode and only when the hero is <= 35m from the enemy tower).

Target group: melee laners only. Both terms are computed only inside `ENABLE_AK_WAVE_AHEAD_VETO && bIsMeleeChar && !bIsJungler && bestEnemyTower` (dllmain.cpp:11066); for ranged bots and junglers bAkWaveAhead and bAkHeroBeyondFront are always false.

Action: none of its own. Both terms are pure vetoes — they suppress AK-Charge / AK-JUMP / Schubser hero writes (and feed the DEFEND-ENGAGE soft-veto exception, see Part 1). They do NOT trigger a retreat; the bot keeps its native target and the laner cascade (MELEE-WAVE-HOLD, escort) decides the position.

Empirical status:
  Wave-Ahead: works as built — it suppresses AK-Charge/Jump in front of the wave, but on its own it moves nothing back. The Schubser bypassed it until Guard A1 (next section).
  HBF (Arm H): mostly idle because of the tier 1/2 gate (packages active only 834/1857s for Sword); 16 suppressed writes, 4/8 held. In Arm AF HBF was the largest F2 blocker (18/52 WHYNOT, also in Defend 60m from the enemy tower) -> HBF inside F2 was then restricted to TO mode + hero <= 35m from the enemy tower (beyondFront blocker 18 -> 2 in Arm AG). Arm AL (RC S-2, MINOR): HBF behind the wave with a hit was the sole blocker in 28 ticks d6-d14 — open, not changed.
  Honest ceiling: the kill exceptions (Wave-Ahead 30 %, HBF 40 %) let the bot chase a low hero into the enemy tower; kill hunts into the enemy tower were seen in 10/15 logs, with one death (d20 T=250, native bInsideEnemyTurretGlobal reported 0 at 6m) — only named, not changed (Arm AR).
Loggers: [AK-VETO] reason=WaveAhead (L1), [SCHUBS-VETO] (archived) (L1, carrier field WaveAhead / WaveAhead+Cnt / Deficit / Outnum), [HERO-BEYOND-FRONT] (archived) (L1, site=AK|SB1|SB2, transitions unthrottled, otherwise 2s).


Schubser Guard 3: `!bAkSoftVeto` (A1, no own switch, active — reaches RANGED bots)
Problem: the Schubser carried four AK discipline channels (Trait-Skip, Kiteback, Death-Zone-Veto, Lane-Push-Veto) but NOT the AK-Soft-Veto. It writes `moveTarget = bestEnemyHero` at two sites that run AFTER MELEE-WAVE-HOLD and after the AK gate, and as the last writer before the Nudge it overrode both the Wave-Ahead veto and MELEE-WAVE-HOLD in the same tick. Measured (debugger7): 36 of 102 MELEE-WAVE-HOLD fires (35 %) were overwritten by an enemy-hero write; example Sword T=515-542 with `AK-VETO(WaveAhead)` + `MELEE-WAVE-HOLD` in every tick and still `mvTgt=BP_Medic_C_0 accR=300 fired=1`, HP 100 -> 26.

Change: one term `&& !bAkSoftVeto` in the OUTER Schubser gate (dllmain.cpp:12437, block dllmain.cpp:12429-12519). It covers both hero writes (Site 1: no native target, hero < 25m and safe-to-push or < 8m -> moveTarget=hero accR 300; Site 2: native target is a minion, hero < 8m (or < 18m for melee with CombatTimer) -> K2_SetEnemy(hero) + moveTarget=hero) and the minion write (enemy minion < 22m -> moveTarget=minion accR 200). bAkSoftVeto carries its own committed-melee exception (hero < 3m), so a started melee exchange is untouched. Revert = delete that one line. In the same block the Hero-Beyond-Front veto suppresses only the two hero writes (sbHeroVeto1/2, Paket 1), and M1 (see Part 1) re-allows the Schubser in Act=Defend when the bot is > 30m from the defended tower.

Target group — correction to the older wording "Melee Schubser": the outer Schubser gate has NO bIsMeleeChar term (dllmain.cpp:12429-12446, identical in beta9); only the 18m sub-branch of Site 2 is melee-gated (dllmain.cpp:12491). The earlier statement in "Ranged Lane-Push-Veto Coverage" that the Melee-Schubser is bIsMeleeChar-gated and unreachable for ranged is therefore not accurate for Site 1 and the < 8m part of Site 2. Because the akv* counters are class-neutral, Guard A1 is live for ranged laners: a ranged bot is no longer pushed toward a hero (< 25m) or an enemy minion (< 22m) while EnM18 > FrM18 + 1 or EnH18 > FrH18 + 1. For ranged, bAkSoftVeto has no Wave-Ahead part (that term is melee-only), and bAkHeroBeyondFront is always false — so a ranged bot CAN still be pushed onto a hero beyond its own wave tip at parity (U1 hypothesis, unmeasured).

Empirical status:
  Melee: the guard is the actual carrier of the Wave-Ahead discipline. Arm U: [SCHUBS-VETO] 134 fires, 84 at parity-or-better, 91 % carrier=WaveAhead, against only 15 suppressed [AK-VETO] events — correcting [AK-VETO] alone would reach 15 % of the events. U3 found no damage argument either way in that log (soft x1.51, ahbf x1.06); keeping the veto at parity is a design ("Soll") decision, not a measured win. Arm AL: Schubser Site 2 was blocked by Soft (32), Act=Defend (20), HBF (12) ticks; no defect.
  Ranged: [SCHUBS-VETO] (archived) lines with the Medic bot as subject exist (Arm V), so the guard demonstrably fires for ranged bots; how often and with what outcome is UNVERIFIED (no ranged contact logger exists, see Part 3).
Logger: [SCHUBS-VETO] (L1, removed, read-only): rebuilds the outer gate WITHOUT the soft term, so it prints exactly the difference set; field carrier=WaveAhead|WaveAhead+Cnt|Deficit+Outnum|Deficit|Outnum, plus Team=/jungler=.


AK-OPPORTUNITY / F2 [AK-OPP] (opportunity punish on a ranged hero, refined several times, active)
Problem: the AK chain is reactive (it needs CombatTimer > 0, i.e. the bot must already have been hit) and is vetoed in front of the wave. Log analysis (debugger6/7) showed Sword used only 24-27 % of real attack opportunities (Medic 3-8m away, Sword with HP advantage); the unused ones were mainly Defend at the tower and escort phases. Competitive play takes these trades. F2 opens a short engage window when a melee laner stands next to a ranged hero it is beating on HP.

Placement: own block dllmain.cpp:13624-13834 (ENABLE_AK_OPPORTUNITY, dllmain.cpp:3382), AFTER all retreat MAINTAIN blocks, P23, ADV-HOLD and DEFEND-EA — it is the last movement writer before the central Nudge (only Q2 Gank-Join, Part 3, follows). Gate `ENABLE_AK_OPPORTUNITY && bIsMeleeChar && !bIsJungler` (dllmain.cpp:13624).

Two modes (akoMode):
  TO  (0): Act=TowerObjective, or Act=Defend far from the defended tower (m1DefFar, M1). Blocked by any Wave-Deficit or heavy deficit (akoBlock "deficit").
  DEF (1): Act=Defend near the defended tower (bot <= 22m, hero <= 26m from it, moveTarget must already be that tower, not in a DE/DLB tick, not in a DPY hold). Since L1 (ENABLE_L1_F2_NO_DEF) the standard DEF entry is switched off ("defOff"): d9-d13 15 DEF windows cost Bot -291 / Hero -98, hero outside 16m in 16/16, and in d13 4/4 DE -> F2-DEF handoffs were lost (Bot -67). Since M5, L1 sits at the END of the DEF chain so that the I2 kill entry still passes (next section).

Entry Conditions (all must hold, dllmain.cpp:13797-13807):
  enemy hero is RANGED (bAkHeroIsRanged = class not in the melee name list)
  hero distance 3-8m (AKO_HERO_MIN_SQ / AKO_HERO_MAX_SQ)
  HP: bot >= 50 % and hero HP <= bot HP, OR (H4) bot >= 60 % and hero HP <= bot HP + 10
  no retreat owner this tick: V44, LPV, Blind, rotation, AK-DZ, StickyDanger, SV / WFB / BR / P23 / ADV-HOLD / DEFEND-EA window (each of these stamps a 2.5s grace, AKO_RETREAT_GRACE_S)
  not: MWH window, generic sticky (lastThreatRetreatTime < 2.5s), Kiteback, Trait-Skip, Hero-Outnumber, Hero-Beyond-Front (TO only, hero <= 35m from the enemy tower), M1 DTY lock-break
  not inside the enemy turret; cooldown 4s since the last CLEAR (AKO_COOLDOWN_S)
  Dive guard (ENABLE_I2_DIVE_ALL, KR-1): not if the hero is <= 35m from ANY standing enemy tower (sweep over AllTowers, dllmain.cpp:13644)
  CombatTimer > 0 — OR (H4) the calm "kill attempt": hero <= 30 % (AKO_CALM_KILL_HP), hero <= 5m, no heavy minion deficit

Action (FIRE): 1x Smart-Redirect K2_SetEnemy(hero) only if the native target is not already the hero; moveTarget = hero, accRadius = 300; bIsActivelyFighting = false, bIsIdle = true, BotIdleTicks = 10; window 3s (AKO_WINDOW_S). It deliberately does NOT set bAntiKiteActive (no AK-JUMP launch), no Blind, no objective write. TO mode additionally writes HeroFocusQuery into 0x0650 (K2, see below).
HOLD re-writes moveTarget/accR each tick. CLEAR on: window expired, mode handoff, hero > 12m (AKO_HERO_CLEAR_SQ), bot < 40 %, hero HP > bot HP + 15 (advLost), or any blocker of the entry list; CLEAR starts the 4s cooldown. With the I2 switches on (default) the CLEAR/extend logic is the I2b variant (next section); for a normal (non-kill) window it is equivalent because ENABLE_I2_TRADE_EXTEND is false.

Target group: melee laners only, and only against RANGED enemy heroes. Junglers and ranged bots never enter.

Empirical validation (human Medic opponent, one match per build, Placebo band ~0.4 per match):
  Arm AF (first F2 build): 5 Sword windows, trade 36:15 for Sword; first human match in which Sword dealt more than he took in contact (ratio 1.16, previous 0.46-0.60), 0 deaths.
  Arm AG: the H4 calm entry was harmful (7 windows, Sword -58 / Medic -17) -> the next build limited the calm entry to the kill attempt above.
  Arm AH: attack quality good (1.31), quantity low — 3 windows (Sword -19 / Medic -8), opportunities used 12/54, killCalm 0.
  Arm AM: 0 F2 fires, all blocked by hpLead (Medic +23.7 HP over Sword) — gate works, no opportunity existed.
  Q3: in the own-tower pendulum all 58/58 openings came from Escort/MFP, AK/AKO/Schubser 0 — F2 is not a lever there.
  Honest ceiling: F2 is a reactive 3s window; it does not create opportunities, and in the measured matches the deciding factor was the HP lead of the human opponent, not the gate.
Loggers: [AK-OPP] FIRE|HOLD|EXTEND|CLEAR (L1, CLEAR with trade balance dHP/dHeroHP over the window, entry=ahead|lead10|killCalm|kill), [AK-OPP-WHYNOT] (archived) (L1, first blocker of an existing opportunity incl. hpLead diagnostics up to hero = bot + 25, AKO_HPLEAD_DIAG).


I2 Kill Window inside F2 (I2a Kill-Entry, I2b Kill-Extend, Dive-All; with M5, active)
Problem: F2 only opens on an HP lead at 3-8m with CombatTimer. Trade Tracker T0 (ENABLE_TRADE_TRACKER, Paket 6, active infrastructure)
Purpose: several decisions need to know whether the bot is currently WINNING or LOSING the exchange with the hero in front of it, not just the HP snapshot. The SOLO1V1 episode anchor (Part 1) had no hero binding and produced swap artifacts. T0 is a small, read-only per-bot trade balance.

Computation (dllmain.cpp:6565-6589; placed BEFORE the recall block so it keeps sampling during recall / flee ticks):
  Gate: bIsMeleeChar && !bIsJungler && maxHealth > 0.
  Ring buffer of 8 samples per bot (time, bot HP %, hero HP %) against the enemy hero within 18m; reset when there is no hero within 18m, the hero changes, or the sample gap exceeds 2.5s.
  trLose3s     = (bot HP % lost) - (hero HP % lost) over the oldest sample at least 3s old (TR_WINDOW_S); positive = losing.
  trHeroLoss3s = hero HP % lost in the same window (Paket 8 K-2a).
  bTradeLosing = trLose3s >= 10 (TR_LOSE_PP; intentionally not the SOLO1V1 threshold of 15 — 0 false alarms in audit S2).
  Unknown is -999 and must NEVER be read as "trade won" (audit S1-B1); all consumers check `!= -999`.
State map g_trState is erased only on dead / despawn (recall paths run every tick, Q4-M2).

Consumers (read-only users, no actuator of its own):
  Defend-Poke-Yield DPY entry / trade-won exit and I1 poke detection (Part 1),
  I2 kill window: entry blocked and window cleared on trLose3s >= 6 (I2_TRADE_LOST); the trade-extension of normal F2 windows (trLose3s <= -3 && trHeroLoss3s >= 3) exists but is switched off (ENABLE_I2_TRADE_EXTEND = false),
  Paket-8 shadow loggers (removed). Consumer lines: DPY dllmain.cpp:9494/9503, I1 dllmain.cpp:9479, I2 dllmain.cpp:13731/13756 (trade-extend term removed).

Target group: melee laners. For ranged bots and junglers the values stay -999, so no trade-based rule can exist for them today (U3: "high" as infrastructure for any ranged trade logic).
Empirical status: Paket 6 as a whole "made nothing worse"; T0 itself has no behavior, its correctness was checked in the audit (0 false alarms) and through its consumers' loggers.

Jungler Lane-Pressure Support

WHY THIS EXISTS — the jungler was never near the lane that was losing:
The legacy Jungler AI (see "Jungler AI" above) picks the nearest camp without any lane term and ganks on a score threshold driven by the odd/even minute clock. Measured on d6-d16: when the solo offlaner was losing its lane, the own jungler stood a median 128-144 m away and farmed the opposite jungle cluster, so a real 2v1 happened 0-1x per match although a 2v1 in that lane was worth roughly +1.6 net HP-points per tick versus -0.75 in the 1v1. Ganks started from more than 80 m reached their target 0/13 times (most of these came from the in-base distance formula, i.e. pure walking time), and the minute clock blocked 41% of the remaining pressure situations. Entwurf Q gives the Macro Brain three things a real jungler does: notice which lane is under pressure, farm on that side of the map, and commit to a gank there when the own laner is in range. It also lets an engaged laner follow the jungler into the 2v1. All building blocks are separate switches (each false = byte-identical), team-neutral (both teams, no team gate), and act only through the existing jungler decision chain (gank target, camp choice, cooldown). The draft (Entwurf Q, REV1-REV5) went through several audits (QA, QB, QL, QZ).

Common gate for Q1b-Q1d (dllmain.cpp:5849):
q1JglOK = bIsJungler && !bDoingBoss && !bLateGame && jungler HP >= 50% (Q1_JGL_MIN_HP @3478) && LastMacroAction != "Recall". The Recall exclusion was added after d17 showed 13 side-camp episodes that were started and erased every tick on the walk home.

Q1a — Gank Distance Cap 80 m (ENABLE_Q1A_GANK_CAP80, @3433):
A gank candidate is only taken over as bestGankTarget if it is within 80 m (Q1_GANK_MAX_SQ @3459), also when the jungler is in base. The currently running gank target is exempt so an ongoing chase is never cut (q1aCapOK @6150-6151, takeover @6155). It removes only ganks that historically never arrived; it adds no writer. Logger [Q1A-CAP] (5 s throttle, removed). Status: kept. Of 95 capped candidates in d17-d20 only 1-3 were later ganked successfully from closer range, i.e. the cap costs nothing measurable.

Q1b — Lane-Pressure Detection (ENABLE_Q1_PRESS_DETECT, @3434; data structure, no behavior by itself):
Computed inside the bot-jungler tick for its own team (dllmain.cpp:5904-6053), so it also protects HUMAN laners (a human has no bot tick of its own). Steps:
  (1) Lane geometry: the T1/T2 towers of both teams are resolved by name (tower_<team>_<left|middle|right>_<outer|inner>) and their positions are cached once per match, so destroyed towers do not vanish from the geometry (@5906-5922).
  (2) Hero-to-lane assignment: a hero belongs to physical lane L if its 2D distance to the lane polyline T2-T1-T1-T2 is <= 20 m (Q1_LANE_W_SQ @3464); own heroes within 50 m of the own core count as "base" and are ignored (@5945-5964). The bot jungler itself is excluded; bots are identified via AllBots/GBotRoles, everything else is the human. Validated live: human 47/50, Sword 40/44, Medic 40/44, geometry bits 12/12 (d17/d18).
  (3) Per lane, n = number of own heroes on that lane. n == 1 (solo rule): pressure if an enemy hero is <= 25 m from the laner and laner HP% <= enemy HP% - 10 (@5975-5986). n >= 2 (duo rule): enemies in contact >= own heroes in contact, sum of own HP% <= sum of enemy HP% - 20, and an enemy hero <= 45 m from the own front tower of that lane (@5987-6014).
  (4) Hysteresis: the condition must hold 5 s continuously to START, an active episode decays 20 s after the last true tick, n == 0 ends it immediately (@6015-6030). State g_q1Press[team][lane] uses ONE team convention (Red 0 / Blue 1 = (int)botTeam), never the playbook teamIdx (known trap, QT P5).
  (5) Priority: only ONE pressure side per jungler (latch g_q1SelLane); the side with the shortest approach wins, a near side is never swapped for a far one, and within 20 m difference the side where the own laner is <= 18 m from the enemy wins (@6031-6052).
Known accepted defects: the duo sum is not normalized by head count, so a lone own ranged hero in contact with two enemies raises false alarms; a normalization (V1-V5) was measured offline and REJECTED (every variant lost 3-10 true episodes to remove 0-2 false alarms). [Q1-PRESS] END lines are missing when the jungler dies.

Q1c — Side Camp under Pressure (ENABLE_Q1C_SIDE_CAMP, @3435):
While a pressure side is selected, a second accumulator in the camp loop picks the living jungle camp closest to that lane's front tower, provided camp-to-tower <= 60 m and jungler-to-camp <= 90 m (~16 s walk; Q1_SIDE_CAMP_TWR_SQ / Q1_SIDE_CAMP_MAX_SQ @3472-3473; accumulator @6223). The swap into bestJungleCreep happens after the camp loop and BEFORE the team-flip block, so the FlippedCreep revert path stays intact (@6238-6251). Guards: a camp the jungler has already started within 20 m is finished first (camp commit, @6245); a chosen side camp is latched until it is cleared or pressure + 20 s expires (g_q1SideLatch, @6242-6247); the farm gate accepts the side camp beyond the legacy 80 m radius (`|| bQ1SideCamp` @8081) so the jungler never falls into the tower fallback; no side camp while ganking. Target group: jungler only (beneficiary: any pressured laner, melee, ranged or human). Status: kept, benefit UNPROVEN. No revert trigger fired (no idling, farm share 55-61% unchanged, 0 deaths at side camps until d21), but the success criterion "jungler <= 70 m of the pressing enemy in >= 60% of cases" was missed (0 / 27 / 56 / 57% in d17-d20, rising). d21 showed the first death in a camp-hero engage at a side camp (Kurohane T=93 at 51% HP) — under observation.

Q1d — Pressure Gank (ENABLE_Q1D_PRESS_GANK, @3436):
q1dEligible (@6058-6070): the candidate is the pressing enemy hero of the selected side, the own laner is <= 18 m from it (hold 25 m once latched), the start distance is <= 60 m (Q1_PRESS_GANK_SQ @3475), and no gank on a different target is running. If eligible: (a) the defense penalty (-30 for more enemy minions than friendly minions at the target) is waived for this target (@6146-6147); (b) the minute clock is overridden — requiredGankScore = 15 even in "farm minutes" and the camp may be interrupted (@7731-7732); (c) a latch g_q1dGankTgt holds the override from gank start to gank end so the gank does not tear between 60 m and the 40 m commitment latch (@7736, cleared @5850). All legacy rules stay: target under its own tower only below 35% HP, 90 s match-time floor, commitment, engage jump. Status: kept. d17-d20 n=14 Q1d ganks: arrival <= 12 s in 71%, net median +6 (enemy HP lost minus own laner HP lost), jungler deaths 2 in 4 matches = exactly at the revert limit, not above it. Decisive in a few episodes (d18 kill T=629), but kill conversion stays weak.

F3 — Short Gank Cooldown without Arrival (ENABLE_Q1E_SHORT_CD_NOARRIVE + Q1E_NOARRIVE_CD_S = 10 s, @3441-3442):
Problem: all ten cooldown writers set 45 s per target, whether or not the jungler had ever reached it. A Q1d gank that ended before arrival (e.g. the laner pulled back > 25 m) locked the target for 45 s while the jungler stood 7-13 m from a 34% enemy under the own tower (d18 T=150-193; three equivalent cases in d13/d14 without Q). Fix: a per-bot marker g_gankArrived is set when GankEngaged becomes true (engage <= 22 m, @7912) and cleared on a new target (@7863), at gank end (@7849) and in the Dead/Despawn/Recall erase lambdas. Only in the gank-end branch the cooldown becomes `arrived ? 45 : 10` s (@7846). Target switch (@7859), interrupt, recall and death paths keep 45 s. This modifies the "45-Second Cooldown Per Target" rule above for the not-arrived case only. Status: kept. d19+d20 n=5 restarts (all only possible through F3): arrival <= 12 s 80%, net median +33, 0 flutter episodes (>= 3 starts on the same target within 60 s), 1 jungler death in 2 matches; d21 0 restarts. Shortest theoretical flutter period by code is 11 s (1 s gank + 10 s).

Q2 — Laner Joins the Engaged Gank (ENABLE_Q2_GANK_JOIN, @3437; block dllmain.cpp:13835-13956):
Purpose: the laner did not exploit the 2v1 when its own jungler arrived (native hero target only 21% of ticks, soft veto and Defend locked it out, nobody read the gank state). Q2 is its own block directly after F2 (AK-OPPORTUNITY) and is the last moveTarget writer before the central Nudge.
Trigger Conditions (all must hold):
  - Melee laner (bIsMeleeChar && !bIsJungler, @13836) with a bestEnemyHero. RANGED LANERS ARE EXCLUDED.
  - Coupling: an own BOT jungler (GBotRoles == 4, same team, alive) with LastMacroAction "Gank", LastGankTargetPtr == this bestEnemyHero, GankEngaged == true, AND the jungler <= 15 m (2D) from the target (@13840-13858). A human jungler never couples.
  - No block (first match is logged as block=): V44 threatened, MELEE-LOCKBREAK active (covers LPV ActiveHard), fleeing, sticky danger retreat, AK death-zone veto, an active retreat-owner window read from the maps at this point (BR, SOLO1V1, WAVE-FALLBACK, DPY, PDY, live LANE-RETURN commit), blind, enemy hero <= 20 m from ANY enemy tower or bot inside enemy turret, own HP <= 35%, BotNeedsHeal, Boss/Rotate/Recall/rotating, enemy heroes within 30 m > friendly heroes within 30 m (2v2 never fires), DEFEND-ENGAGE locked this tick (@13862-13894). Defend is deliberately allowed.
  - Geometry: enemy hero <= 10 m for 2 ticks to enter, <= 15 m to hold, 3 s latch on the geometry only (@13895-13904).
Action: K2_SetEnemy(hero) only when the native target is not the hero AND (start, hero change, or >= 2 s since the last call) — never per tick (@13907-13914); moveTarget = hero, accR 300, bIsIdle = true, BotIdleTicks 10; HeroFocusQuery written to bot+0x0650 inside the block; g_deEngagedLastTick = false so the DEFEND-ENGAGE cleanup after the Nudge does not undo the K2 (@13916-13921).
Status: kept, rare. d17-d20: 3 fires, net median +15, 0 tower dives, 0 deaths; d18 first fire (Chains R) target 81% -> dead, net +87. d17 showed 0 fires / 7 blocks (laner 114-174 m away). Ranged laners could in principle join from 15-17 m without diving, but the radii (10/15 m) are melee values — out of scope, hypothesis only.

Q3 — No SOLO1V1-VETO for Junglers (ENABLE_Q3_SV_NO_JUNGLER, @3438):
The SOLO1V1 fire gate (dllmain.cpp:11363) gains `!(ENABLE_Q3_SV_NO_JUNGLER && bIsJungler)`. Reason (R2/R3): the SV gate had no jungler exclusion and accepted Act=Jungle; camp damage counted as a "lost trade", so a jungler at 100% HP was blinded and sent to a lane tower 23-57 m away (d11-d16 ~7 episodes, 1 death after release). With Q3 the jungler's farm/fallback writer stays the last writer and the AK-DZ escape can reach the jungler again exactly as before beta9. Status: kept (0 SV lines with R=4 in d17-d21).

Gank Execution — Approach, Combat Lock and Target Choice (clarification, code check 2026-10-05)
(Line numbers refer to the published Server/dllmain.cpp (14,716 lines) and shift with later edits; grep the switch or logger name if a number no longer fits. The gank code itself is unchanged since beta5 except the F3 marker and the blindSrc logger tag)

This section corrects two impressions that older notes and the "Engage Jump" paragraph above can give: that the jungler rushes in blind, and that it holds a fixed gank target.

Approach phase (target > 22 m; engage hysteresis enter 22 m / exit 30 m, dllmain.cpp:7905-7911):
The gank branch writes moveTarget = bestGankTarget, accR 100, BotIdleTicks 10 and sets the flag bIsRotatingToObjective = true with blindSrc = "Gank" (@7913-7918). It does NOT call K2_SetIgnoreEnemies(true). The Iron Rule block (@10582-10586) only switches engine blindness OFF, never on. The jungler therefore runs to the target SEEING — the native combat AI can still be distracted on the way (how often: UNVERIFIED). The old comment "rushes BLIND (IgnoreEnemies)" was based on the flag shown in a logger, not on an engine call. Verified identical in beta5. Across the code, the gank approach and the rotation (@9776) are the only two places that set the blind flag without engine blindness.

Combat lock while the gank runs:
  - EQS: for LastMacroAction == "Gank" the EQS override selects HeroFocusQuery as the desired query for bot+0x0650 every tick, in approach and engage alike; it is the first branch of the override cascade (dllmain.cpp:12339-12340).
  - Objective BT: on engage ObjectiveBT (0x50) is switched from BT_GoToObjective to BT_AttackObjective (@7928-7930), the jungle combat BT under which the native service picks the hero instead of a minion.
  - Engine target: Smart-Redirect — K2_SetEnemy(gank hero) is called only when the native target (GetEnemyCharacter) is not the gank hero (@7932-7944); when it already is, the code keeps its hands off so ability casts are not interrupted (per-tick K2_SetEnemy would block abilities, see Known Limitations). On engage an existing blind flag is cleared with K2_SetIgnoreEnemies(false) (@7923-7926).
So the "lock" is HeroFocus + Smart-Redirect on whatever target the gank branch selected in this tick — it is a lock of the native combat AI onto the gank target, not a lock of the gank target itself.

Target choice — no target fidelity:
The gank scan selects bestGankTarget = highest score every tick (@6155-6160). The only exemption for the running target is the Q1a 80 m cap. bAlreadyGanking (@7727, target < 40 m and not under its tower unless < 35% HP) keeps the gank STATE alive, but it evaluates the new score maximum, not the previous target. On a target change the old target receives a 45 s cooldown (@7857-7859). Measured d17-d21 (S1): 44 "[Q1-GANK] END why=switch" lines, of which only 23 are real switches (21 are a logger artifact: the jungler at 1-25% HP enters a heal/flee path, the target pointer is nulled while LastMacroAction is still "Gank"). Real switches are almost always a symptom of the old target escaping (tower 10, recall 2, free escape 2); all 12 surviving kill-near old targets escaped. Counterfactual target fidelity (12-22 m / 30-50% HP variants): without a tower exception about -0.2 kills per match plus extra tower dives, with a tower exception -0.07..0.00 kills per match. Verdict: no draft — the switch is the symptom; the actual gap is pursuit without finish at equal movement speed (4 low-HP chases in d17-d21 where the jungler stayed 1-7 m from the target for 10-22 s without a kill).

Engage Jump (unchanged since beta5, dllmain.cpp:7946-8010):
Behavior as documented above (engaged, not NeedsHeal/tactical recall, not blind, target 3-18 m, 3 s cooldown, LaunchToPoint ~1 m in front of the hero, max 6 m, wall raycast, landing-point tower guard 18 m). Which guard blocks the jump in the low-HP pursuit cases (tower guard while the target flees to its tower, NeedsHeal because ganks start at low jungler HP, target < 3 m, or plain equal speed after landing) is UNVERIFIED: [GANK-JUMP] (launch and skip reasons) and [GANK-LOCK] are compiled only at F4_LOG_LEVEL >= 2, and [GANK-JUMP] logs the blue team only.

A flank approach (behind the target, between target and its tower) was evaluated and REJECTED: open flank at arrival only 5/40 lane ganks, ceiling +0.4 kills per match, realistic ~0.1; it would also need a new actuator (MoveToLocation, never used in the DLL).
